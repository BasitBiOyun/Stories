# A2 TEACHER GUIDE  
## for the Interactive Storybook: Prophet Abraham

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

### Purpose of the Book

This interactive digital storybook helps A2 learners develop English through story, visuals, audio, hotspots, guided discussion, and short interactive checks. It keeps students active, supported, and emotionally engaged while learning about Prophet Abraham (as), his search for truth, his courage, and his trust in Allah.

### Target Learners

- Primary: Turkish middle school students studying English around CEFR A2  
- Secondary: Other lower-intermediate learners using values-based story content

### Language Level

**CEFR A2**

The language should remain clear, repetitive, concrete, and classroom-friendly. New vocabulary should be meaningful and useful, but should not overload learners.

### Main Learning Focus

- Reading and listening fluency  
- Basic story comprehension  
- Core thematic vocabulary  
- Guided speaking and reflection  
- Values and character education  
- Interactive support for understanding

### Educational Value

The story gives learners age-appropriate English for discussing:

- belief in one God  
- truth and false worship  
- courage  
- trust in Allah  
- patience  
- family and guidance  
- struggle and perseverance  
- gratitude

### Digital Support

The digital format reduces cognitive load and increases engagement.

- Hotspots support difficult words and concepts  
- Audio supports pronunciation and rhythm  
- Interactive checks give immediate comprehension support  
- Visuals help weaker learners stay included

## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT

This guide supports a holistic classroom approach by combining language learning, structured participation, and values reflection.

### Language Skills

- **Listening & Viewing:** students follow guided audio and visual cues  
- **Reading Comprehension:** students move from literal understanding to simple inference  
- **Spoken Interaction:** students answer short questions, compare ideas, and retell events  
- **Vocabulary Development:** students meet useful thematic words in context and recycle them during discussion

### Personal and Social Development

- **Values Education:** courage, trust, patience, sincerity, gratitude, obedience, kindness  
- **Social-Emotional Learning:** identifying fear, hope, sadness, bravery, and determination  
- **Reflective Thinking:** connecting story lessons to real life

## 3. TEACHING APPROACH

The teaching approach should remain simple, supportive, interactive, and appropriate for A2 learners.

### Core Principles

- Pre-teach only a few key words  
- Use visuals first, then audio, then text  
- Move from “What happened?” to “Why did it happen?”  
- Use pair talk before whole-class answers  
- End each lesson with a short real-life bridge  
- Keep teacher talk clear and short  
- Repeat important language in natural ways  

### Recommended Classroom Sequence

1. **See it** – students look at visuals  
2. **Hear it** – students listen to short chunks  
3. **Read it** – students follow the text  
4. **Say it** – students answer or retell  
5. **Connect it** – students link the lesson to life  

### Teacher Role

The teacher should act as a guide, not as a lecturer. The aim is not to explain everything for a long time, but to help students notice, understand, speak, and reflect.

## 5. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER READING FRAMEWORK

### Before Reading

- Use the cover or main illustration for prediction  
- Teach 4–6 key words  
- Ask curiosity questions  
- Activate prior knowledge with short, safe prompts  

### During Reading

- Play or read short chunks  
- Pause for gist questions  
- Encourage meaning guesses before using support  
- Use pair talk before calling on individuals  
- Recycle target words during the lesson  

### After Reading

- Ask one values question  
- Ask one personal connection question  
- Finish with a short written or oral exit task

## Chapter 1: Searching for the True Creator

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- identify 4–5 key words related to idols, stars, moon, sun, and Creator  
- answer simple comprehension questions about Abraham’s search for truth  
- say at least one sentence about why stone idols cannot help people  
- use simple present and can/can’t language in meaningful context  

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- say who Abraham was  
- explain one reason why Abraham did not worship idols  
- use one or two target words in a sentence  

### Key Vocabulary

idol, star, moon, sun, cave, Creator, truth, pray, worship, guide

### Pedagogical Focus

Use discovery learning and visual comparison. Let students notice the difference between created things and the Creator through images, short questions, and repeated language.

### Grammar Focus

**Can / Can’t** and simple present for facts

- Idols can’t hear.  
- Idols can’t help people.  
- Allah creates everything.  

### Pronunciation Focus

- final consonants in **star**, **moon**, **sun**  
- stress in **creator**, **worship**, **guidance**

### Materials / App Features

- early pages of the story  
- star, moon, sun visuals  
- selected hotspots  
- audio playback  
- simple vocabulary matching or oral comparison task  

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
- Ask: “What do people worship?”  
- Ask: “Can a stone object hear people?”

**5–10 min | Visual Prediction**  
- Show the page or illustration  
- Ask students to guess the topic using visuals  

**10–15 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
- Teach 4–5 key words with gestures, visuals, or board support  

**15–23 min | Guided Reading / Listening**  
- Read or play the text in short chunks  
- Pause briefly after each chunk  

**23–28 min | Quick Comprehension Check**  
- Ask short CCQs  
- Let students answer in pairs first  

**28–33 min | Pair Task**  
- Students compare idols and Allah using can/can’t sentences  

**33–37 min | Reflection**  
- Ask: “Why did Abraham keep thinking?”  
- Ask: “Why is truth important?”  

**37–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
- Write one new word  
- Write one fact learned today  

### Teacher Language

- “Look at the picture. What can you see?”  
- “Listen first. Do not worry about every word.”  
- “Can this object hear people?”  
- “Talk to your partner.”  
- “Can you answer in one sentence?”  

### Comprehension Questions

- What did people worship?  
- Why did Abraham think idols were silly?  
- Why were the star, moon, and sun not Allah?  

### Discussion Points

- Why can’t idols help people?  
- Why did Abraham keep searching for the truth?  
- Why is Allah different from created things?  

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may know the story idea but not the English words  
- The difference between Creator and creation may feel abstract  
- Some students may answer only with one word  

### Support Strategies

- Use sentence frames: “Abraham did not worship ___ because ___.”  
- Accept short answers first, then expand them  
- Use visuals and gestures before translation  

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write 3 can/can’t sentences about idols and Allah  
- **Struggling learners:** match visuals to words such as star, moon, sun, idol  

### Interactive App Tips

- Use hotspots on heavenly objects to compare what appears and disappears  
- Use the idol hotspot to discuss why humans should not worship objects  

### Assessment Tools

- Focus on vocabulary, comprehension, and participation  
- Exit ticket: one fact and one word  

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students move to picture stations for idol, star, moon, sun, and Creator ideas, then say one key word or one short sentence at each stop.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can clear thinking help people avoid wrong ideas?

### Homework / Extension

- Listen again to one short section  
- Write 2 sentences using new words  
- Ask a family member: “Why is truth important?”

## Chapter 2: Abraham’s Courage and the Fire

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain how Abraham spoke to his father and people  
- identify the main events about the broken idols and the fire  
- describe Abraham’s courage and trust in Allah in simple English  
- use simple past verbs to retell part of the story  

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- say what Abraham did in the idol house  
- explain why the people became angry  
- give one example of Abraham’s courage  

### Key Vocabulary

festival, axe, break, angry, fire, rope, safe, miracle, trust, king

### Pedagogical Focus

Use event sequencing and cause-and-effect discussion. Students should see how one brave action led to strong reactions from the people.

### Grammar Focus

**Past simple verbs**

- broke  
- said  
- went  
- threw  
- came  
- became  

### Pronunciation Focus

- past simple endings in **asked**, **looked**, **played**  
- stress in **miracle**, **festival**, **courage**

### Materials / App Features

- middle pages of the story  
- sequencing cards or board support  
- fire miracle visuals  
- relevant hotspots  
- short retelling activity  

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Review**  
- Quick recap from the previous chapter  
- Ask 2 simple recall questions  

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Lead-in**  
- Teach or review break, axe, fire, miracle, safe, trust  

**10–18 min | Reading / Listening**  
- Focus on the idol house, people’s anger, and the fire  
- Pause after important moments  

**18–25 min | Sequencing Task**  
- Put main events in order  
- Use first, then, after that  

**25–30 min | Speaking Practice**  
- Students retell the story in pairs with 3–4 past simple verbs  

**30–35 min | Reflection**  
- Ask: “Why was Abraham brave?”  
- Ask: “Why did he trust Allah?”  

**35–38 min | Mini Debate / Quick Talk**  
- “Was Abraham afraid?”  
- “Can a person be afraid and brave at the same time?”  

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
- Write one past tense verb  
- Write one sentence about Abraham’s courage  

### Teacher Language

- “What happened first?”  
- “Why were the people angry?”  
- “Use a past tense verb.”  
- “Why did Abraham stay calm?”  
- “Tell me one reason.”  

### Comprehension Questions

- Why did Abraham break the idols?  
- Why did he leave the biggest idol unbroken?  
- What happened in the fire?  
- Why was the fire a miracle?  

### Discussion Points

- Why was Abraham brave?  
- Why did the people refuse to accept the truth?  
- What does trust in Allah mean?  

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may confuse the order of events  
- Past simple verbs may be inaccurate  
- Some students may focus only on the miracle and not on the message  

### Support Strategies

- Use a sequencing strip or timeline  
- Give sentence starters: “First… Then… After that…”  
- Model one short retelling before pair work  

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write advice for Nimrod or the people  
- **Struggling learners:** label event pictures in order  

### Interactive App Tips

- Use the biggest idol hotspot to discuss Abraham’s smart answer  
- Use the fire hotspot to emphasize miracle, safety, and trust in Allah  

### Assessment Tools

- Focus on sequencing, vocabulary, and past tense use  
- Exit ticket: one sentence about courage  

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students walk to event cards in order and say one sentence at each stop: idol house, broken idols, angry people, fire, miracle.

### Global Citizenship Link

Why is it important to stand for truth even when others disagree?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 3 past simple sentences about the story  
- Draw the main events and label them  
- Ask at home: “What makes a person brave?”

## Chapter 3: Journey, Zamzam, and the Ka‘ba

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- identify the main events in Abraham’s journey with Hagar and Ishmael  
- explain the story of Zamzam and the building of the Ka‘ba in simple English  
- talk about patience, trust, and gratitude  
- use simple future or purpose language in context  

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- say where Abraham took Hagar and Ishmael  
- explain how Zamzam appeared  
- say why the Ka‘ba is important  

### Key Vocabulary

desert, valley, hill, water, protect, pray, blessing, build, Ka‘ba, pilgrim

### Pedagogical Focus

Use emotional understanding and values-based reflection. Students should see the story not only as an event sequence but also as a lesson in trust, effort, and gratitude.

### Grammar Focus

**Purpose and future meaning**

- Abraham asked Allah to protect his family.  
- Hagar ran to find water.  
- Abraham and Ishmael were going to build the Ka‘ba.  

### Pronunciation Focus

- long vowel sounds in **water**, **valley**, **Ka‘ba**  
- stress in **protect**, **blessing**, **important**

### Materials / App Features

- later pages of the story  
- Zamzam and Ka‘ba visuals  
- map or place visuals if available  
- relevant hotspots  
- short speaking or matching task  

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
- Ask: “What do people need in a desert?”  
- Ask: “What would you do if you had no water?”  

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
- Teach desert, valley, hill, water, blessing, build  

**10–18 min | Guided Reading**  
- Read in small chunks  
- Pause for gist understanding  

**18–24 min | Comprehension Check**  
- Ask short questions about Hagar, Ishmael, and Zamzam  

**24–30 min | Pair Task**  
- Students explain the story with picture support  

**30–35 min | Values Reflection**  
- Ask: “Why did Hagar not give up?”  
- Ask: “Why should people be grateful?”  

**35–38 min | Quick Speaking**  
- Students say one sentence about the Ka‘ba and one sentence about Zamzam  

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
- Write one lesson from Hagar’s story  
- Write one important place name  

### Teacher Language

- “Where were Abraham, Hagar, and Ishmael?”  
- “Why did Hagar run?”  
- “What happened near Ishmael?”  
- “Why is the Ka‘ba important?”  
- “Say one lesson from the story.”  

### Comprehension Questions

- Where did Abraham leave Hagar and Ishmael?  
- Why did Hagar run between the hills?  
- What was Zamzam?  
- Why did Abraham and Ishmael build the Ka‘ba?  

### Discussion Points

- What does trust in Allah look like?  
- Why is patience important?  
- Why should people be grateful for blessings?  

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may mix the desert events and the Ka‘ba events  
- Some vocabulary may be unfamiliar  
- Students may know place names but not be able to explain them in English  

### Support Strategies

- Use visuals and a simple event timeline  
- Give sentence frames: “Hagar ran because…” / “Zamzam was…”  
- Recycle key words several times during the lesson  

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a short thank-you paragraph about blessings  
- **Struggling learners:** match key places and words to pictures  

### Interactive App Tips

- Use the Zamzam hotspot to discuss Allah’s help and blessing  
- Use the Ka‘ba hotspot to explain its importance in simple English  

### Assessment Tools

- Focus on comprehension, vocabulary, and speaking effort  
- Exit ticket: one lesson and one key place  

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students move between picture stations for valley, hill, water, Zamzam, and Ka‘ba, then say one key word or one short sentence at each station.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can gratitude and trust help people in difficult times?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 3 simple sentences about Zamzam or the Ka‘ba  
- Listen again and repeat 5 short lines  
- Ask at home: “Why is gratitude important?”

## 7. CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT

### Recommended Use Modes

- **Whole-Class Mode:** teacher projects the story and controls the pace  
- **Pair Work Mode:** one student reads, one student supports with hotspots or explanation  
- **Quiz Mode:** use teams for motivation, not pressure  
- **Exit Ticket Mode:** close each lesson with a short written check  

### Practical Notes

- Keep transitions quick  
- Do not read too much without stopping  
- Let weaker students rehearse in pairs before public speaking  
- Keep hotspot use purposeful

## 9. ASSESSMENT & RUBRICS

### Assessment Evidence

- participation in discussion  
- use of target vocabulary  
- comprehension responses  
- sequencing accuracy  
- oral retelling  
- exit tickets  
- short written reflection  

### What to Look For

- Can the student follow the main story line?  
- Can the student use a few target words correctly?  
- Can the student answer simple questions in English?  
- Can the student connect the lesson to a value or personal idea?  

### Master Rubric (A2)

| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Good (3) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | Uses several new words correctly | Uses some new words | Uses very few new words |
| Comprehension | Retells key events clearly | Understands the main idea | Needs support to retell |
| Grammar | Uses target structures with control | Makes some errors | Uses limited structure |
| Reflection | Makes a clear personal or value connection | Gives a simple idea | Needs prompting |
| Participation | Joins actively and appropriately | Participates sometimes | Rarely participates |

## 13. SENSITIVE NOTES

- Present false worship in a clear but age-appropriate way  
- Handle the fire scene calmly and without dramatic fear language  
- Present Nimrod as an arrogant ruler, not as a heroic rival  
- Keep language age-appropriate and non-graphic  
- Focus on lesson, consequence, and trust in Allah  

---

## 14. ENGLISH TIPS FOR TEACHERS

- Keep classroom English simple and repeatable  
- Support meaning with visuals, gesture, repetition, and sentence frames  
- Praise effort specifically  
- Keep instructions short and consistent  
- Recycle target words across lessons  
- Use Turkish only briefly when truly needed for abstract meaning  
- Return student production to English whenever possible  

### Useful Classroom Language

- “Listen and follow.”  
- “Work with your partner.”  
- “Say one sentence.”  
- “What happened first?”  
- “Why do you think that?”  
- “Use the new word in your answer.”
