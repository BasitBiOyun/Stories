## for the Interactive Storybook: Prophet Abraham

## 1. WELCOME

Hello!

Welcome to your **B2 Self-Study Guide** for **The Story of Prophet Abraham**.

This guide will help you use your interactive storybook in a thoughtful and independent way. The story is rich in meaning, so your goal is not only to read the events. Your goal is also to understand ideas, notice values, learn new vocabulary, improve your English, and reflect on the message of the story.

You can use this guide:

- before class  
- after class  
- while studying alone  
- while reviewing a chapter  
- before a speaking or writing task  
- when preparing a project or presentation  

You do not need to understand every difficult word immediately. At B2 level, you should learn how to guess from context, use support tools wisely, and explain your ideas clearly.

Go step by step. Read carefully. Think deeply. Use English actively.

## 3. WHAT IS IN THIS BOOK?

This book tells the story of Prophet Abraham (as), including his search for truth, his rejection of idolatry, his debates with his people, the fire miracle, his meeting with Nimrod, his migration, Hajar and Ishmael, Zamzam, the sacrifice, and the building of the Ka‘ba.

In the book, you will find:

- long reading sections  
- listening support  
- visuals and page design  
- key vocabulary  
- clickable word help  
- interactive comprehension checks  
- chapter review activities  
- reflection questions  
- opportunities for speaking and writing  

This means you are not only reading a story. You are also practicing English through analysis, reflection, and communication.

## 5. HOW TO USE THIS BOOK

Use the book in this order whenever possible.

### Step 1: Preview the page

Before reading, look at:

- the title  
- the image  
- the names  
- highlighted words  
- any hotspot symbols  
- the general mood of the page  

Ask yourself:

- What part of the story is this?  
- What may happen here?  
- What do I already know?  
- What words or ideas may be important?  

### Step 2: Listen for the main idea

Listen once without stopping too much.

Try to understand:

- who is involved  
- what is happening  
- what the problem is  
- what feeling or value is important  

Do not worry about every word during the first listening.

### Step 3: Read slowly and actively

Read in short sections. After each section, pause and ask:

- What happened?  
- Why did it happen?  
- What does this show about the character?  
- Which sentence is important?  

At B2 level, active reading is important. Do not only move your eyes across the text. Think while reading.

### Step 4: Use hotspots wisely

When you see a difficult word or concept:

1. Try to guess the meaning from context.  
2. Click the hotspot or word help.  
3. Read the explanation.  
4. Return to the sentence.  
5. Say the meaning in your own words.  

Do not click every word immediately. First try to understand the main idea.

### Step 5: Mark key lines

Choose 1–3 important lines from a chapter.

A key line may show:

- Abraham’s reasoning  
- a major event  
- a value such as trust or patience  
- a turning point  
- a lesson  

Use these lines later in speaking or writing.

### Step 6: Do the activities

After reading, complete the interactive activities carefully.

Before answering:

- reread the question  
- think about the chapter  
- check the text if needed  
- avoid guessing too quickly  

### Step 7: Read the feedback

Feedback is part of learning. If your answer is wrong:

- read the explanation  
- go back to the text  
- find the correct evidence  
- try again if possible  

### Step 8: Review before moving on

Before closing the book, do a quick review:

- write 3–5 important words  
- say one sentence aloud  
- write one short summary sentence  
- answer one reflection question

## 6. A SIMPLE STUDY ROUTINE

Small, regular study is better than rushing through a long text.

### A. Short Study Plan (10–15 Minutes)

Best for quick review.

**What to do:**

1. Open one short section.  
2. Look at the image and title.  
3. Listen once.  
4. Read the text slowly.  
5. Click 2–3 important hotspots.  
6. Write 3 key words.  
7. Say one sentence about the main idea.  

### B. Normal Study Plan (25–30 Minutes)

Best for regular self-study.

**What to do:**

1. Preview the section.  
2. Listen once for the main idea.  
3. Read carefully.  
4. Note 5 new or important words.  
5. Complete the activities.  
6. Check feedback.  
7. Write a 3–4 sentence summary.  
8. Write one reflection sentence.  

### C. Deep Study Plan (40–50 Minutes)

Best for weekend review, project preparation, or exam-style practice.

**What to do:**

1. Study one full chapter or story block.  
2. Listen twice.  
3. Read and underline key lines.  
4. Create a vocabulary list.  
5. Complete all activities.  
6. Write a paragraph summary.  
7. Answer one discussion question in writing.  
8. Say a one-minute oral summary.

## 7. STUDY BLOCK 1  
### Tawheed, Hanifism, and Abraham’s Mission

In this part, you learn why Prophet Abraham (as) is presented as a central figure of monotheism. You also meet important terms such as Tawheed, Hanifism, idolatry, and mission.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What does it mean to believe in Allah alone?  
- Why is Prophet Abraham (as) important in Islamic tradition?  
- What is the difference between sincere belief and blind imitation?  

### While you read

Look for:

- how Abraham (as) is introduced  
- what Tawheed means  
- what Hanifism means  
- why idolatry is rejected  
- how Abraham’s mission is described  

### Key words

Tawheed, Hanifism, monotheism, idolatry, idolater, mission, faith, guidance

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Write a one-sentence definition of Tawheed.  
2. Write a one-sentence definition of Hanifism.  
3. Explain why Abraham (as) is connected with monotheism.  
4. Write 3 key words and use each one in a sentence.  

### Self-check

- Can I explain Tawheed in English?  
- Can I say why Abraham’s mission matters?  
- Can I use the words monotheism and idolatry correctly?  

### Reflection

- Why is it important for belief to be sincere?  
- Why should people think carefully about what they follow?

## 8. STUDY BLOCK 2  
### Childhood, Idols, and Critical Thinking

In this part, Abraham (as) observes idols and understands that they are lifeless and powerless. His childhood scenes show that he questions false beliefs from an early age.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- Can something made by people become a god?  
- Why do people sometimes respect objects too much?  
- What is the difference between tradition and truth?  

### While you read

Look for:

- what Abraham’s father does  
- how Abraham reacts to the idols  
- why the statue of Mardukh seems ridiculous to him  
- what people do inside the temple  
- what Abraham understands about lifeless objects  

### Key words

idol, statue, temple, worship, lifeless, helpless, offering, bow, beg, reasoning

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Make a two-column chart: “What people believed” and “What Abraham noticed.”  
2. Write three cause-effect sentences using because, therefore, or as a result.  
3. Explain why Abraham rejected idols.  
4. Choose one important sentence and explain it.  

### Self-check

- Can I retell Abraham’s childhood scene?  
- Can I explain why idols are powerless?  
- Can I use cause-effect language?  

### Reflection

- Why is critical thinking important?  
- How can people question false ideas respectfully?

## 9. STUDY BLOCK 3  
### The Sky, Creation, and the True Creator

In this part, Abraham (as) reflects on the star, the moon, and the sun. He sees that they appear and disappear. He understands that created things cannot be the Creator.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What changes in the sky every day?  
- Why can something that changes not be Allah?  
- How can observation help a person think deeply?  

### While you read

Look for:

- the order: star, moon, sun  
- what Abraham says or thinks each time  
- what happens to each heavenly body  
- what conclusion Abraham reaches  
- how reasoning leads to certainty  

### Key words

Creator, creation, appear, disappear, set, command, certainty, heavenly body, evidence

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Complete this table: object / what Abraham saw / what happened / conclusion.  
2. Write three contrast sentences using although, however, or but.  
3. Explain the difference between a sign and the Creator of the sign.  
4. Say a one-minute summary aloud.  

### Self-check

- Can I explain why Abraham rejected the star, moon, and sun as objects of worship?  
- Can I use the words Creator and creation correctly?  
- Can I give a clear conclusion?  

### Reflection

- How can nature remind people of Allah?  
- Why is deep thinking important in faith?

## 13. HOW TO LEARN NEW WORDS

At B2 level, vocabulary learning is not only memorizing definitions. You should learn how words work in context.

### Good vocabulary steps

1. Write the word.  
2. Guess its meaning from the sentence.  
3. Check the hotspot or dictionary if needed.  
4. Write a short definition in your own words.  
5. Copy the sentence from the story.  
6. Write your own sentence.  
7. Review the word later in a new context.  

### Example

**Word:** arrogance  
**Meaning:** thinking too highly of oneself and refusing truth  
**Story sentence idea:** The people were too arrogant to accept Abraham’s message.  
**My sentence:** Arrogance can stop a person from admitting a mistake.

### Best words to collect from this book

- Tawheed  
- Hanifism  
- monotheism  
- idolatry  
- reasoning  
- evidence  
- arrogance  
- trust  
- sacrifice  
- obedience  
- mercy  
- gratitude  
- pilgrimage  
- sanctuary

## 14. HOW TO IMPROVE LISTENING

Use the audio more than once.

### First listening: main idea

Listen without stopping. Ask:

- Who is in this part?  
- What is the main event?  
- What is the problem?  
- What feeling is important?  

### Second listening: text connection

Listen while reading. Notice:

- pronunciation  
- sentence rhythm  
- key words  
- repeated phrases  
- emotional tone  

### Third listening: speaking practice

Pause after important sentences and repeat them aloud. Try to imitate pronunciation and rhythm.

### Listening tips

- Use headphones if possible  
- Do not stop for every unknown word  
- Focus on meaning first  
- Replay only difficult parts  
- Write down 2–3 useful expressions

### Questions to ask while reading

- What happened?  
- Why did it happen?  
- What does this show?  
- What is the evidence?  
- What value appears here?  
- How is this event connected to earlier events?

## 17. HOW TO WRITE DURING SELF-STUDY

Write short but clear answers. At B2 level, try to support your ideas.

### Easy writing tasks

- write a 3-sentence summary  
- write 5 new words and sentences  
- answer one “why” question  
- compare two characters  
- explain one value  
- write one paragraph reflection  

### Paragraph structure

Use this simple structure:

1. Topic sentence  
2. Event or evidence from the story  
3. Explanation  
4. Reflection or conclusion  

### Good writing prompts

- Why did Abraham reject idol worship?  
- How did Abraham use reasoning to guide his people?  
- Why was the fire miracle important?  
- How did Nimrod misunderstand power?  
- What does Hajar’s story teach about trust and effort?  
- Why is the Ka‘ba important in Abraham’s story?

## 18. WHAT TO DO WHEN A PART FEELS DIFFICULT

Difficulty is normal in a B2 text. Do not stop immediately.

Try this:

1. Pause.  
2. Look at the title and image again.  
3. Listen to the audio once more.  
4. Read only one paragraph.  
5. Identify the subject, verb, and main idea.  
6. Click only the most important hotspots.  
7. Write one simple sentence about the paragraph.  
8. Continue slowly.  

If it is still difficult:

- divide the chapter into smaller parts  
- read with a classmate or family member  
- ask your teacher one focused question  
- return to the text later  

Remember: understanding a difficult text step by step is real progress.
