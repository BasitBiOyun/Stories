## 1. OVERVIEW

### Purpose of the Book
This interactive digital storybook helps learners develop English through story, visuals, audio, hotspots, guided discussion, and short interactive checks. It aims to keep students active, supported, and emotionally engaged during reading.

### Target Learners
- **Primary:** Turkish middle school students studying English around CEFR A2.
- **Secondary:** Other lower-intermediate learners using values-based story content.

### Language Level
**CEFR A2**

The language should stay clear, repetitive, concrete, and classroom-friendly. New vocabulary should be meaningful but not overload students.

### Main Learning Focus
- Reading and listening fluency
- Basic story comprehension
- Core thematic vocabulary
- Guided speaking and reflection
- Values and character education
- Interactive support for understanding

### Educational Value
The story gives learners age-appropriate English for discussing:
- creation
- knowledge
- humility
- regret
- jealousy
- responsibility
- right and wrong choices

### Digital Support
The digital format reduces cognitive load and increases engagement.
- Hotspots support difficult words and concepts.
- Audio supports pronunciation and rhythm.
- Interactive checks give immediate comprehension support.
- Visuals help weaker learners stay included.

## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT

This guide supports a holistic classroom approach by combining language learning, structured participation, and values reflection.

### Language Skills
- **Listening & Viewing:** students follow guided audio and visual cues.
- **Reading Comprehension:** students move from literal understanding to simple inference.
- **Spoken Interaction:** students answer short questions, compare ideas, and retell events.
- **Vocabulary Development:** students meet useful thematic words in context and recycle them during discussion.

### Personal and Social Development
- **Values Education:** humility, sincerity, responsibility, patience, regret, self-control, peaceful behavior.
- **Social-Emotional Learning:** identifying feelings, discussing consequences, making better choices.
- **Reflective Thinking:** connecting story lessons to real life.

## 3. TEACHING APPROACH

The teaching approach should remain simple, supportive, interactive, and appropriate for A2 learners.

### Core Principles
- Pre-teach only a few key words.
- Use visuals first, then audio, then text.
- Move from “What happened?” to “Why did it happen?”
- Use pair talk before whole-class answers.
- End each lesson with a short real-life bridge.
- Keep teacher talk clear and short.
- Repeat important language in natural ways.

### Recommended Classroom Sequence
1. **See it** - students look at visuals.
2. **Hear it** - students listen to short chunks.
3. **Read it** - students follow the text.
4. **Say it** - students answer or retell.
5. **Connect it** - students link the lesson to life.

### Teacher Role
The teacher should act as a guide, not as a lecturer. The aim is not to explain everything for a long time, but to help students notice, understand, speak, and reflect.

## 5. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER READING FRAMEWORK

### Before Reading
- Use the cover or main illustration for prediction.
- Teach 4–6 key words.
- Ask curiosity questions.
- Activate prior knowledge with short, safe prompts.

### During Reading
- Play or read short chunks.
- Pause for gist questions.
- Encourage meaning guesses before using support.
- Use pair talk before calling on individuals.
- Recycle target words during the lesson.

### After Reading
- Retell the events in order.
- Ask one values question.
- Ask one personal connection question.
- Finish with a short written or oral exit task.

## 6. CHAPTER SUPPORT (40-MINUTE LESSONS)

> These three instructional modules are the A2 teaching blocks for the Adam unit. They are designed for direct classroom use and can be followed as written or adapted slightly to class level.

---

### Chapter 1: Creation & Knowledge
**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:
- identify 4–5 key words related to creation and knowledge
- answer simple comprehension questions about the beginning of Adam’s story
- say at least one sentence about why knowledge is important
- notice and use simple future meaning with **be going to**

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:
- say who Adam is
- explain one thing that makes humans special in the story
- use one or two target words in a sentence

#### Key Vocabulary
soil, human, knowledge, earth, messenger, learn, curiosity

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use discovery learning. Let students notice visuals and selected hotspots first so that reading begins with curiosity instead of explanation.

#### Grammar Focus
Future with **be going to**
- Allah was going to create a human.
- People are going to learn many things.

#### Pronunciation Focus
- /ə/ sound in *messenger*, *curiosity*
- stress in multi-syllable words

#### Materials / App Features
- first pages of the story
- selected hotspots
- audio playback
- simple vocabulary activity or matching

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–5 min | Warm-up**
- Ask: “Who was the first human?”
- Ask: “What do you already know?”

**5–10 min | Visual Prediction**
- Show the page or illustration.
- Ask students to guess the topic using visuals.

**10–15 min | Vocabulary Preview**
- Teach 4–5 key words with gestures, visuals, or board support.

**15–23 min | Guided Reading / Listening**
- Read or play the text in short chunks.
- Pause briefly after each chunk.

**23–28 min | Quick Comprehension Check**
- Ask short CCQs.
- Let students answer in pairs first.

**28–33 min | Pair Task**
- Matching, sentence completion, or oral response.

**33–37 min | Reflection**
- Ask: “Why is knowledge important?”
- Ask: “How should people use knowledge?”

**37–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one new word.
- Write one fact learned today.

#### Teacher Language
- “Look at the picture. What can you see?”
- “Listen first. Do not worry about every word.”
- “Talk to your partner.”
- “What do you think this word means?”
- “Can you answer in one sentence?”

#### Comprehension Questions
- Who was going to be created?
- What was Adam created from?
- Why is knowledge important in this story?

#### Discussion Points
- What makes humans special according to the story?
- Why is knowledge important?
- How should a person use knowledge?

#### Possible Difficulties
- Students may understand the story idea but struggle to explain it in English.
- The concept of “knowledge” may become too abstract.
- Some students may know the content in Turkish but not the English words.

#### Support Strategies
- Use short sentence frames: “Humans are special because…”
- Accept short answers first, then expand them.
- Use visuals and gestures before translation.

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** write 3 sentences about how humans should care for the world.
- **Struggling learners:** match key words to visuals.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the soil hotspot to explain humble origin.
- Use the light or knowledge hotspot to discuss learning.

#### Assessment Tools
- Focus on vocabulary, comprehension, and participation.
- Exit ticket: one fact and one word.

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Students move to picture stations about earth, nature, and responsibility, then say one key word or one simple sentence at each station.

#### Global Citizenship Link
If all humans come from one beginning, how should we treat each other?

#### Homework / Extension
- Listen again to one short section.
- Write 2 sentences using new words.
- Ask a family member: “Why is learning important?”

### Chapter 2: arrogance vs Humility
**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:
- compare Adam and Iblis using simple adjectives and comparatives
- explain the meanings of arrogance, humility, and regret in simple English
- give one example of a good reaction after a mistake
- use at least one comparative sentence correctly

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:
- say one difference between Adam and Iblis
- use a comparison such as “more humble” or “better than”
- explain why saying sorry matters

#### Key Vocabulary
proud, humble, regret, mistake, sorry, better, worse, arrogant

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use character comparison and guided discussion. A Venn diagram, T-chart, or comparison grid works well.

#### Grammar Focus
Comparatives
- wiser than
- better than
- more humble than

#### Pronunciation Focus
Word stress in *arrogant*, *superior*, *regret*

#### Materials / App Features
- chapter pages with key contrast points
- comparison board/chart
- relevant hotspots
- short speaking task

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–5 min | Review**
- Quick recap from the previous chapter.
- Ask 2 simple recall questions.

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Lead-in**
- Teach or review proud, humble, regret, mistake, sorry.

**10–18 min | Reading / Listening**
- Focus on the key dialogue or narration.
- Pause after important moments.

**18–25 min | Compare Characters**
- Fill in a chart: Adam / Iblis.
- Use simple contrast language.

**25–30 min | Speaking Practice**
- Students use comparative sentences in pairs.

**30–35 min | Role-play**
- One student speaks proudly.
- The other responds with humility or apology language.

**35–38 min | Reflection**
- Ask: “What should we do after a mistake?”

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one comparison sentence.
- Write the opposite of arrogance.

#### Teacher Language
- “Who is acting with arrogance?”
- “Who is more humble?”
- “Tell me one difference.”
- “What should a person do after a mistake?”
- “Use a comparative sentence.”

#### Comprehension Questions
- Why was arrogance a problem?
- How did Adam respond differently?
- Why is saying sorry important?

#### Discussion Points
- Why is arrogance dangerous?
- Why is saying sorry important?
- What is the difference between a mistake and arrogance?

#### Possible Difficulties
- Students may confuse “mistake” with “bad character.”
- Comparative forms may be inaccurate.
- Some students may give very short answers without explanation.

#### Support Strategies
- Give sentence frames: “Adam was more ___ than ___.”
- Model one correct comparison before pair work.
- Allow oral rehearsal before writing.

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** create a short apology dialogue.
- **Struggling learners:** underline comparative words in the text.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the thinking or mind hotspot to discuss human thought.
- Use the sorrow or regret hotspot to emphasize feelings after mistakes.

#### Assessment Tools
- Focus on character understanding, grammar accuracy, and speaking effort.
- Exit ticket: one comparison sentence.

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Mirror game: one student shows an arrogant pose, the other a humble pose, then both explain the difference.

#### Global Citizenship Link
How can humility reduce conflict in real life?

#### Homework / Extension
- Write 3 comparative sentences.
- Complete a character comparison chart.
- Ask at home: “Why is saying sorry important?”

### Chapter 3: Habil and Qabil
**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:
- identify the main events of Habil and Qabil in sequence
- explain the ideas of sincerity, jealousy, and consequence in simple English
- retell part of the story using past simple verbs
- give one peaceful solution to anger or conflict

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:
- put the main events in order
- use 2–3 past simple verbs in retelling
- state one moral lesson from the story

#### Key Vocabulary
offer, sincere, jealous, crow, bury, angry, peaceful, lesson

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use values-based teaching with calm, age-appropriate discussion. Focus on lesson and consequence, not graphic detail.

#### Grammar Focus
Past simple verbs
- brought
- offered
- killed
- cried
- learned

#### Pronunciation Focus
/v/ and /b/ in words like *valuable* and *behave*

#### Materials / App Features
- chapter pages
- sequencing activity
- crow hotspot
- offering visuals
- short retelling task

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–4 min | Warm-up**
- Compare two gifts.
- Ask: “Which gift is more sincere?”

**4–10 min | Vocabulary Preview**
- Teach offer, sincere, jealous, crow, bury.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading**
- Read in small chunks.
- Pause for gist understanding.

**18–24 min | Sequencing Task**
- Put the main events in order.

**24–30 min | Discussion**
- Why was one offering accepted?
- What is jealousy?

**30–35 min | Language Practice**
- Retell part of the story using past simple verbs.

**35–38 min | Values Reflection**
- Ask: “How can we stop anger before it grows?”

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one lesson from the crow.
- Write one peaceful action.

#### Teacher Language
- “Let’s put the events in order.”
- “What happened first?”
- “Why was one offering accepted?”
- “What can people do instead of hurting others?”
- “Use a past tense verb.”

#### Comprehension Questions
- What did the two brothers offer?
- Why was one offering accepted?
- What did the crow teach?

#### Discussion Points
- What is real goodness?
- Why is jealousy dangerous?
- What can people do instead of hurting others?

#### Possible Difficulties
- The emotional content may affect some students.
- Students may know the moral idea but not the language to express it.
- Past simple verb forms may be inconsistent.

#### Support Strategies
- Keep language calm and non-graphic.
- Use a sequencing strip or board support.
- Provide sentence starters: “First… Then… After that…”

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** write advice for Qabil.
- **Struggling learners:** draw or label the main events.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the crow hotspot to discuss learning from nature.
- Use the offering visuals to compare sincerity and effort.

#### Assessment Tools
- Focus on moral understanding, sequencing, and past tense use.
- Exit ticket: one moral lesson.

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Students walk to event cards in order and say one sentence at each stop.

#### Global Citizenship Link
Discuss peaceful conflict resolution and self-control.

#### Homework / Extension
- Write 3 past simple sentences about the story.
- Draw the main events and label them.
- Ask at home: “What should people do when they feel jealous?”

## 7. CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT

### Recommended Use Modes
- **Whole-Class Mode:** teacher projects the story and controls the pace.
- **Pair Work Mode:** one student reads, one student supports with hotspots or explanation.
- **Quiz Mode:** use teams for motivation, not pressure.
- **Exit Ticket Mode:** close each lesson with a short written check.

### Practical Notes
- Keep transitions quick.
- Do not read too much without stopping.
- Let weaker students rehearse in pairs before public speaking.
- Keep hotspot use purposeful.

## 8. DIFFERENTIATION

### For Support
- Use visuals first.
- Ask yes/no or either/or questions.
- Allow short L1 support for abstract ideas.
- Repeat key sentences together.
- Use sentence starters.

### For Extension
- Ask for diary entries.
- Ask “why” questions with sentence frames.
- Let students create a values poster.
- Ask students to give advice in English.

### For Mixed Ability
- Use peer support.
- Give optional challenge tasks.
- Keep core success simple and reachable for everyone.

## 9. ASSESSMENT & RUBRICS

### Assessment Evidence
- participation in discussion
- use of target vocabulary
- comprehension responses
- sequencing accuracy
- oral retelling
- exit tickets
- short written reflection

### What to Look For
- Can the student follow the main story line?
- Can the student use a few target words correctly?
- Can the student answer simple questions in English?
- Can the student connect the lesson to a value or personal idea?

### Master Rubric (A2)
| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Good (3) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | Uses several new words correctly | Uses some new words | Uses very few new words |
| Comprehension | Retells key events clearly | Understands the main idea | Needs support to retell |
| Grammar | Uses target structures with control | Makes some errors | Uses limited structure |
| Reflection | Makes a clear personal or value connection | Gives a simple idea | Needs prompting |
| Participation | Joins actively and appropriately | Participates sometimes | Rarely participates |

## 13. SENSITIVE NOTES

- Present Adam’s mistake as a learning moment.
- Handle the death of Habil calmly and carefully.
- Present Iblis as a warning example of arrogance.
- Keep language age-appropriate and non-graphic.
- Focus on lesson, consequence, and better choices.

## 14. ENGLISH TIPS FOR TEACHERS

- Keep classroom English simple and repeatable.
- Support meaning with visuals, gesture, repetition, and sentence frames.
- Praise effort specifically.
- Keep instructions short and consistent.
- Recycle target words across lessons.
- Use Turkish only briefly when truly needed for abstract meaning.
- Return student production to English whenever possible.

### Useful Classroom Language
- “Listen and follow.”
- “Work with your partner.”
- “Say one sentence.”
- “What happened first?”
- “Why do you think that?”
- “Use the new word in your answer.”
