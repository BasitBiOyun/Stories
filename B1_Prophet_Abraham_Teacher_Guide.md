# B1 TEACHER GUIDE  
## for the Interactive Storybook: Prophet Abraham

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

### Purpose of the Book

This interactive digital storybook helps B1 learners develop English through story, visuals, audio, hotspots, guided discussion, vocabulary work, comprehension checks, speaking tasks, writing tasks, and values-based reflection.

The story of Prophet Abraham gives students meaningful language for discussing Tawheed, truth, courage, trust in Allah, patience, migration, gratitude, and sincere worship. The digital format supports comprehension while keeping learners active and emotionally engaged.

### Target Learners

- Primary: Turkish middle school or early high school students studying English around CEFR B1
- Secondary: Other intermediate learners using values-based story content

### Language Level

**CEFR B1**

The language can include longer sentences, cause-and-effect relationships, simple reasoning, and more abstract values vocabulary. However, teacher support should keep the lesson clear, structured, and accessible.

### Main Learning Focus

- Reading and listening fluency
- Narrative comprehension
- Cause-and-effect understanding
- Thematic vocabulary development
- Guided speaking and retelling
- Short paragraph writing
- Character and values reflection
- Interactive support for independent learning

### Educational Value

The story gives learners age-appropriate English for discussing:

- belief in one God
- the difference between Creator and creation
- rejecting false worship
- courage and speaking the truth
- trust in Allah during difficulty
- arrogance and refusal of truth
- migration for a mission
- patience, effort, and gratitude
- the significance of Zamzam, Mecca, and the Ka’ba

### Digital Support

The digital format reduces cognitive load and increases engagement.

- Hotspots support difficult words and concepts
- Audio supports pronunciation, rhythm, and fluency
- Interactive checks give immediate comprehension support
- Visuals help weaker learners follow the story
- Reflection tasks help stronger learners express deeper ideas

## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT

This guide supports a holistic classroom approach by combining language learning, structured participation, critical thinking, and values reflection.

### Language Skills

- **Listening & Viewing:** students follow guided audio, visual cues, and story sequence
- **Reading Comprehension:** students identify main ideas, supporting details, cause and effect, and simple inference
- **Spoken Interaction:** students answer questions, compare ideas, retell events, and explain lessons
- **Vocabulary Development:** students meet useful thematic words in context and recycle them in speech and writing
- **Writing:** students produce short summaries, reflection sentences, comparison paragraphs, and cause-and-effect explanations

### Personal and Social Development

- **Values Education:** Tawheed, courage, sincerity, trust, patience, gratitude, obedience, humility
- **Social-Emotional Learning:** identifying fear, anger, arrogance, shame, hope, effort, and determination
- **Reflective Thinking:** connecting story lessons to daily life and moral decision-making
- **Global Citizenship:** discussing respect, truthfulness, responsibility, and shared human values

## 3. TEACHING APPROACH

The teaching approach should remain clear, supportive, interactive, and appropriate for B1 learners.

### Core Principles

- Pre-teach only the most important vocabulary
- Use visuals first, then audio, then text
- Move from “What happened?” to “Why did it happen?” and “What does it teach?”
- Use pair talk before whole-class answers
- Ask students to support answers with story evidence
- Recycle vocabulary across activities
- Balance comprehension, language practice, and values reflection
- Keep teacher talk focused and purposeful

### Recommended Classroom Sequence

1. **See it** – students observe visuals and predict
2. **Hear it** – students listen for gist
3. **Read it** – students read for detail
4. **Explain it** – students answer why/how questions
5. **Use it** – students practice vocabulary or grammar
6. **Retell it** – students summarize or sequence events
7. **Connect it** – students reflect on the lesson

### Teacher Role

The teacher should act as a guide and facilitator. The aim is not to explain the whole story for students, but to help them notice meaning, build language, discuss values, and express ideas with increasing independence.

## 4. IMPLEMENTATION PLANS

### Option A: Intensive Workshop (80 Minutes)

- 0–10 min: Warm-up, visual prediction, and key vocabulary
- 10–30 min: Guided reading/listening of selected sections
- 30–45 min: Vocabulary and hotspot exploration
- 45–60 min: Sequencing, comprehension, or quiz task
- 60–75 min: Speaking/writing reflection
- 75–80 min: Exit ticket and review

### Option B: Standard Split (3 x 40 Minutes)

- Lesson 1: Abraham in Babylon and his discovery of the Creator
- Lesson 2: Calling people, breaking the idols, and the fire miracle
- Lesson 3: Nimrod, migration, Hagar, Zamzam, Mecca, and the Ka’ba

### Option C: Extended Project (5–6 Lessons)

- Deep reading of each story block
- Vocabulary recycling and personal word bank
- Character comparison: Abraham, people, Nimrod, Hagar, Ishmael
- Interactive sequencing and comprehension review
- Speaking project: retell the story with visuals
- Writing project: values paragraph or story reflection

## 5. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER READING FRAMEWORK

### Before Reading

- Use the cover or main illustration for prediction
- Teach 5–7 key words
- Ask curiosity questions
- Activate prior knowledge with safe prompts
- Introduce one guiding question for the section

### During Reading

- Play or read short chunks
- Pause for gist and detail questions
- Ask students to identify cause and effect
- Encourage meaning guesses before using support
- Use pair talk before calling on individuals
- Recycle target words during the lesson
- Ask students to underline evidence in the text

### After Reading

- Retell the events in order
- Ask one cause-and-effect question
- Ask one values question
- Ask one personal connection question
- Finish with a short written or oral exit task

## Chapter 1: Abraham and the World of Idols

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- identify key vocabulary related to idols, statues, temple, and worship
- explain why Abraham rejected idol worship
- describe Abraham’s early environment in Babylon
- use cause-and-effect language to explain simple ideas

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- say what people in Babylon worshipped
- explain why statues could not be real gods
- describe Abraham’s father’s job
- use at least three target words in sentences

### Key Vocabulary

idol, statue, temple, worship, bow, beg, favor, respect, hatred, intelligent

### Pedagogical Focus

Use discovery learning and visual comparison. Help students notice the contradiction between human-made statues and real divine power.

### Grammar Focus

**Cause and effect**

- Abraham rejected idols because they could not help anyone.
- People worshipped statues, so Abraham tried to show them the truth.
- The idols could not hear; therefore, they could not answer prayers.

### Pronunciation Focus

- stress in **intelligent**, **worship**, **respect**
- final consonants in **idol**, **temple**, **statue**

### Materials / App Features

- opening pages of the story
- idol and temple visuals
- selected hotspots
- audio playback
- vocabulary matching or sentence completion task

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
Ask: “What can a statue do? What can it not do?”

**5–10 min | Visual Prediction**  
Show the image of Babylon, idols, or the temple. Students predict the problem.

**10–15 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
Teach 5–6 key words with visuals and examples.

**15–23 min | Guided Reading / Listening**  
Read or play the opening section in short chunks.

**23–28 min | Comprehension Check**  
Ask short questions about people’s beliefs and Abraham’s reaction.

**28–33 min | Pair Task**  
Students complete sentences: “An idol cannot ___, so it cannot ___.”

**33–37 min | Reflection**  
Ask: “Why is it important to question wrong ideas?”

**37–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
Write two things idols cannot do and one reason Abraham rejected them.

### Teacher Language

- “Look at the picture. What can you see?”
- “What did people believe?”
- “What did Abraham notice?”
- “Use because or so in your answer.”
- “Can you support your answer from the story?”

### Comprehension Questions

- What did people in Babylon worship?
- What was Abraham’s father’s job?
- Why did Abraham think idols were powerless?

### Discussion Points

- Why do people sometimes follow traditions without thinking?
- Why was Abraham different from the people around him?
- What is the difference between respect and worship?

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may know the story but not the English vocabulary
- The idea of idol worship may require careful explanation
- Students may give one-word answers instead of explanations

### Support Strategies

- Provide sentence frames: “Abraham rejected idols because …”
- Use visuals before translation
- Accept short answers first, then expand them

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a short paragraph explaining why idols cannot be gods
- **Struggling learners:** match key words to visuals

### Interactive App Tips

- Use hotspots on idols/statues to reinforce cannot hear, cannot move, cannot help
- Use audio replay for difficult paragraphs

### Assessment Tools

- Vocabulary use
- Cause-and-effect sentence
- Exit ticket

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students stand in two corners: “can” and “cannot.” The teacher says actions such as hear, move, speak, help, and students choose the correct corner for idols.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can careful thinking protect people from false ideas?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 5 sentences using cannot
- Create a word map for worship and idol
- Ask a family member: “Why should people think carefully before following a tradition?”

## Chapter 2: Abraham Discovers the Creator

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain Abraham’s reasoning about the star, moon, and sun
- distinguish Creator from creation in simple B1 English
- use contrast language such as but, however, and although
- answer inference questions about guidance and truth

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- retell Abraham’s observation of the sky
- explain why created things cannot be Allah
- use contrast words correctly in at least two sentences

### Key Vocabulary

Creator, creation, prostrate, guide, path, fade, disappear, sunrise, sunset, messenger

### Pedagogical Focus

Use guided reasoning. Students should notice the logic of the passage: things that appear and disappear cannot be the Creator.

### Grammar Focus

**Contrast language**

- The star was bright, but it disappeared.
- Although the sun was big, it was still a creation.
- The moon shone beautifully; however, it faded.

### Pronunciation Focus

- stress in **Creator**, **creation**, **disappear**
- vowel sounds in **guide**, **bright**, **night**

### Materials / App Features

- sky visuals
- star, moon, and sun hotspots
- audio playback
- comparison chart

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
Ask: “What changes in the sky during one day?”

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
Teach Creator, creation, disappear, guide, and path.

**10–18 min | Reading / Listening**  
Read/listen to the star, moon, and sun section.

**18–25 min | Reasoning Chart**  
Students complete: object / what happened / Abraham’s conclusion.

**25–30 min | Language Practice**  
Students write contrast sentences using but, although, however.

**30–35 min | Discussion**  
Ask: “Why did Abraham ask Allah for guidance?”

**35–38 min | Reflection**  
Ask: “How can thinking carefully help a person find truth?”

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
Write one contrast sentence and one sentence about the Creator.

### Teacher Language

- “What did Abraham see first?”
- “What changed?”
- “What conclusion did he reach?”
- “Use however in your answer.”
- “What is the difference between Creator and creation?”

### Comprehension Questions

- What did Abraham see at night?
- Why was the star not Allah?
- What did Abraham understand when the sun set?

### Discussion Points

- Why is Allah different from created things?
- Why is guidance important?
- How does the story show the value of reflection?

### Possible Difficulties

- Creator/creation may be abstract
- Students may confuse sequence of star, moon, and sun
- Contrast connectors may be used incorrectly

### Support Strategies

- Use a visual chart
- Model one sentence before pair work
- Provide connector frames

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a 4-sentence explanation of Abraham’s reasoning
- **Struggling learners:** complete sentence stems with but and because

### Interactive App Tips

- Use sky hotspots to compare appearing and disappearing
- Replay the audio for rhythm and pronunciation

### Assessment Tools

- Reasoning chart
- Contrast sentence
- Exit ticket

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students hold cards: star, moon, sun, Creator. They move in sequence and explain which things appear/disappear and which does not.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can observation and reasoning help people make better decisions?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 3 contrast sentences
- Draw the sequence of star, moon, and sun
- Write a 3-sentence answer: “What did Abraham learn?”

## Chapter 3: Calling People, Breaking the Idols, and the Fire

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- identify the main events from Abraham’s call to the fire miracle
- explain the people’s reaction using feelings vocabulary
- retell events using past simple verbs and sequencing language
- discuss courage and trust in Allah

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- put the events in order
- explain why the people became angry
- describe the fire miracle
- use at least five past tense verbs in retelling

### Key Vocabulary

furious, arrogant, ashamed, axe, destroy, untouched, punish, firewood, flames, miracle, trust

### Pedagogical Focus

Use sequencing and values-based discussion. Keep the lesson focused on courage, truth, arrogance, and trust rather than only the dramatic event.

### Grammar Focus

**Past simple and sequencing**

- First, Abraham called his people.
- Then, he broke the idols.
- After that, the people questioned him.
- Finally, Allah made the fire cool and safe.

### Pronunciation Focus

- past simple endings in **asked**, **looked**, **trusted**
- stress in **arrogant**, **miracle**, **furious**

### Materials / App Features

- idol-breaking visuals
- fire miracle visuals
- sequencing cards
- relevant hotspots
- audio playback

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Review**  
Ask two questions from Chapters 1–2.

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Lead-in**  
Teach/review axe, arrogant, ashamed, flames, miracle, trust.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading / Listening**  
Focus on Abraham’s call, the broken idols, and the people’s anger.

**18–25 min | Sequencing Task**  
Students place event cards in order.

**25–30 min | Retelling Practice**  
Students retell the events in pairs using first, then, after that, finally.

**30–35 min | Values Discussion**  
Ask: “Why was Abraham calm?” and “Why did the people refuse the truth?”

**35–38 min | Mini Writing**  
Students write two sentences about courage and trust.

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
Write one past tense verb and one lesson from the fire miracle.

### Teacher Language

- “What happened first?”
- “Why were the people furious?”
- “What did Abraham leave untouched?”
- “Use a past tense verb.”
- “What does trust in Allah mean here?”

### Comprehension Questions

- Why did Abraham break the idols?
- Why did he leave the biggest statue untouched?
- What did the people realize?
- How did Allah save Abraham?

### Discussion Points

- Can a person be afraid and brave at the same time?
- Why is arrogance dangerous?
- Why did the miracle not change the people’s hearts?

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may confuse event order
- Past tense forms may be inaccurate
- Students may focus only on the miracle and miss the moral lesson

### Support Strategies

- Use a timeline on the board
- Provide sentence starters
- Recast errors gently during speaking

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a short reflection on courage
- **Struggling learners:** match events to pictures before retelling

### Interactive App Tips

- Use the biggest idol hotspot to discuss Abraham’s answer
- Use the fire hotspot to emphasize safety, miracle, and trust

### Assessment Tools

- Sequencing accuracy
- Past tense use
- Exit ticket

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students stand in a line as event cards and physically arrange themselves in the correct sequence.

### Global Citizenship Link

Why is it important to stand for truth even when many people disagree?

### Homework / Extension

- Write a 6-sentence retelling
- Learn 5 past simple verbs from the chapter
- Ask at home: “What makes a person brave?”

## Chapter 4: Abraham and Nimrod, Then Migration

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain Nimrod’s claim and Abraham’s response
- identify the logic in Abraham’s argument
- discuss migration for a mission
- use reported speech or summary language in simple form

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- summarize the debate with Nimrod
- explain why Nimrod could not answer
- say why Abraham left Babylon
- write a short summary paragraph

### Key Vocabulary

king, ordinary, death, life, power, response, naturally, faith, migration, message

### Pedagogical Focus

Use critical thinking and debate analysis. Students should see that Abraham’s answer is simple, logical, and powerful.

### Grammar Focus

**Reported speech / summary language**

- Nimrod said that he could give life and death.
- Abraham answered that Allah makes the sun rise in the east.
- Abraham realized that people would not listen.

### Pronunciation Focus

- stress in **naturally**, **migration**, **ordinary**
- clear pronunciation of **life** and **death**

### Materials / App Features

- Nimrod dialogue section
- debate chart
- migration map or route visual if available
- audio playback

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
Ask: “What is real power?”

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
Teach power, response, faith, migration, message.

**10–18 min | Reading / Listening**  
Read/listen to the Nimrod section.

**18–24 min | Debate Chart**  
Students complete: Nimrod’s claim / Abraham’s answer / result.

**24–30 min | Speaking Practice**  
Students explain the debate to a partner in 3 sentences.

**30–35 min | Migration Discussion**  
Ask why Abraham left Babylon and what he wanted to spread.

**35–38 min | Mini Writing**  
Students write a summary paragraph of the debate.

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
Write one sentence about real power and one about migration.

### Teacher Language

- “What did Nimrod claim?”
- “How did Abraham answer?”
- “Why was this answer strong?”
- “Summarize the debate in your own words.”
- “Why did Abraham leave Babylon?”

### Comprehension Questions

- Who was Nimrod?
- What did Nimrod say he could do?
- What did Abraham ask Nimrod to do?
- Who believed with Abraham?
- Why did Abraham migrate?

### Discussion Points

- What is the difference between power and arrogance?
- Why is logic useful when explaining truth?
- What can people leave behind for a higher purpose?

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may retell the debate too literally without explaining the logic
- Reported speech may be new for some learners
- Migration may need emotional context

### Support Strategies

- Use a three-column debate chart
- Give sentence frames: “Nimrod claimed that …”
- Accept direct speech first, then convert to summary language

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a short dialogue between Abraham and Nimrod
- **Struggling learners:** complete a cloze summary

### Interactive App Tips

- Use any king or debate hotspot to discuss arrogance and power
- Use a route/map visual for migration if available

### Assessment Tools

- Debate chart
- Summary paragraph
- Exit ticket

### Kinesthetic Activity

Two students stand as “claim” and “answer.” Other students give evidence cards and decide which side has real logic.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can respectful debate help people understand truth?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 4 reported speech sentences
- Create a migration timeline
- Ask at home: “Why do people sometimes leave their homes for an important mission?”

## Chapter 5: Hagar, Ishmael, Zamzam, Mecca, and the Ka’ba

**Timing:** 40 minutes

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- identify the main events in the valley, Zamzam, Mecca, and the Ka’ba
- explain Hagar’s effort and trust in Allah
- describe the importance of Zamzam and the Ka’ba
- use purpose language in meaningful context

### Expected Student Outcomes

Students should be able to:

- say where Hagar and Ishmael stayed
- explain why Hagar ran between Safa and Marwa
- explain how Zamzam appeared
- say why the Ka’ba is important
- write one lesson about effort and gratitude

### Key Vocabulary

valley, desert, protect, blessing, thirst, spring, sacred, settle, foundation, construct, pilgrimage

### Pedagogical Focus

Use emotional understanding and values-based reflection. Students should see Hagar’s story as a lesson in trust, effort, patience, and gratitude.

### Grammar Focus

**Purpose language**

- Abraham prayed to protect his family.
- Hagar ran to find water.
- People settled there because of the spring.
- Abraham and Ishmael built the Ka’ba for worship.

### Pronunciation Focus

- stress in **foundation**, **pilgrimage**, **gratitude**
- clear pronunciation of **valley**, **sacred**, **Zamzam**

### Materials / App Features

- desert/valley visuals
- Safa and Marwa visuals
- Zamzam and Ka’ba visuals
- map support if available
- audio playback
- sequencing or matching activity

### 40-Minute Lesson Flow

**0–5 min | Warm-up**  
Ask: “What do people need most in a desert?”

**5–10 min | Vocabulary Preview**  
Teach valley, thirst, spring, sacred, foundation, construct.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading / Listening**  
Read/listen to the Hagar and Zamzam section.

**18–24 min | Comprehension Check**  
Ask short questions about Hagar, Ishmael, and Zamzam.

**24–30 min | Sequencing / Picture Task**  
Students put valley, search, Zamzam, settlement, Ka’ba in order.

**30–35 min | Values Reflection**  
Ask: “How did Hagar show both trust and effort?”

**35–38 min | Speaking Practice**  
Students say one sentence about Zamzam and one about the Ka’ba.

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**  
Write one purpose sentence and one gratitude sentence.

### Teacher Language

- “What was the problem in the valley?”
- “What did Hagar do?”
- “Why did people settle there?”
- “Use to in your purpose sentence.”
- “What should people be grateful for?”

### Comprehension Questions

- Where did Abraham leave Hagar and Ishmael?
- What did Abraham pray for?
- Why did Hagar run between the hills?
- How did Zamzam appear?
- What did Abraham and Ishmael build?

### Discussion Points

- Why is effort important together with trust?
- What makes Zamzam special in the story?
- Why is the Ka’ba important for Muslims?
- How does Hajj remember Abraham’s family?

### Possible Difficulties

- Students may struggle with place names
- The Hajj/Umrah vocabulary may be unfamiliar
- Students may describe events without explaining values

### Support Strategies

- Use a simple map or visual sequence
- Provide sentence frames: “Hagar ran to …”
- Keep religious vocabulary clear and respectful

### Differentiation

- **Fast finishers:** write a paragraph about trust and effort
- **Struggling learners:** label pictures with key words

### Interactive App Tips

- Use the Zamzam hotspot to discuss blessing and gratitude
- Use the Ka’ba hotspot to connect worship, unity, and Abraham’s mission

### Assessment Tools

- Event sequence
- Purpose sentence
- Exit ticket

### Kinesthetic Activity

Students walk between two classroom points labeled Safa and Marwa, then explain Hagar’s effort in one sentence.

### Global Citizenship Link

How can patience and effort help communities grow?

### Homework / Extension

- Write 5 sentences about Hagar’s effort
- Draw a simple timeline from the valley to the Ka’ba
- Ask at home: “Why should people be grateful for blessings?”

## 9. ASSESSMENT IDEAS

### Formative Assessment

- vocabulary matching
- quick comprehension checks
- pair retelling
- exit tickets
- one-minute oral summaries
- cause-and-effect sentences
- short reflection answers

### Summative Assessment

Students may complete one of these:

- a 150–180 word story summary
- a values paragraph about courage, trust, or patience
- a character comparison
- a timeline poster
- a short oral retelling with visuals

### Simple Rubric

| Area | Excellent | Good | Needs Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comprehension | Understands events and reasons clearly | Understands main events | Needs help with sequence/details |
| Vocabulary | Uses many target words correctly | Uses some target words | Uses few target words |
| Speaking | Retells clearly with connectors | Retells with some support | Needs sentence frames |
| Writing | Writes clear connected sentences | Writes understandable sentences | Needs structure and vocabulary help |
| Reflection | Explains values deeply | Gives a simple lesson | Needs guiding questions |

## 10. FINAL PROJECT OPTIONS

### Option 1: Story Timeline

Students create a timeline of Prophet Abraham’s story with 8–10 events and short captions.

### Option 2: Values Poster

Students choose one value: courage, trust, patience, gratitude, or sincerity. They explain how the story teaches it.

### Option 3: Character Reflection

Students write a paragraph comparing Abraham and Nimrod, or Hagar’s effort and the people’s arrogance.

### Option 4: Oral Retelling

Students retell the story in 2–3 minutes using pictures or key words.

---

## 11. TEACHER NOTES

- Keep the story respectful, age-appropriate, and focused on learning.
- Use visuals and audio to support weaker learners.
- Encourage students to answer in full sentences at B1 level.
- Do not over-explain every vocabulary item.
- Let students discover meaning through context first.
- Return often to the main values: Tawheed, courage, trust, patience, gratitude, and sincerity.

---

## 12. FINAL MESSAGE FOR TEACHERS

This story offers rich language and deep values. With careful guidance, students can improve their English while reflecting on truth, courage, trust, effort, gratitude, and worship.

The goal is not only to finish the story. The goal is to help students understand it, speak about it, write about it, and carry its lessons into life.
