# B1 SELF-STUDY GUIDE  
## for the Interactive Storybook: Prophet Abraham

---

## 1. WELCOME

Hello!

Welcome to your **B1 Self-Study Guide** for **The Story of Prophet Abraham**.

This guide will help you use the interactive storybook in a focused, independent, and meaningful way. You can use it at school, at home, before class, after class, or when you want to review the story by yourself.

This book is not only for reading. It is also for listening, vocabulary learning, thinking, speaking, writing, checking your understanding, and reflecting on important values.

At B1 level, you do not need to understand every single word immediately. However, you should try to understand the main idea, follow the order of events, notice cause and effect, and explain the lessons of the story in your own words.

This guide will show you:

- how to study each part of the story
- how to read and listen more actively
- how to learn and recycle new vocabulary
- how to use interactive activities carefully
- how to check your mistakes
- how to summarize and retell the story
- how to connect the story to real life

Go step by step. Read carefully, think deeply, and keep improving.

---

## 2. WHO IS THIS GUIDE FOR?

This guide is for:

- B1 English learners
- students in middle school or early high school
- students who want to study with or without a teacher
- students who want to improve reading, listening, speaking, and writing through a values-based story
- students who already understand simple stories but want to express deeper ideas

This guide is useful if:

- you can understand the main points of a story but sometimes need support with details
- you want to learn religious and moral vocabulary in English
- you want to explain events, reasons, feelings, and lessons more clearly
- you want to use the storybook as a real study tool, not only as a reading text

This guide is your learning companion inside the book.

## 5. HOW TO USE THIS BOOK

Use the book in this order.

### Step 1: Preview the section

Look at the title, picture, and layout.

Ask yourself:

- What do I already know about this part?
- Who may appear in this section?
- What problem or lesson may come next?

### Step 2: Listen for the main idea

Listen once without stopping.

Ask:

- Who is this part about?
- What is the main event?
- Is the mood calm, tense, sad, hopeful, or surprising?

### Step 3: Read actively

Read the text slowly. Do not translate every word. Try to follow the meaning in English.

While reading, notice:

- important actions
- feelings
- reasons
- results
- repeated words
- values

### Step 4: Use word help wisely

When you see a difficult word:

1. Try to guess the meaning from the sentence.
2. Click or check the word help.
3. Read the sentence again.
4. Say the word aloud.
5. Write a short sentence with it.

Do not stop for every word. Focus first on the story.

### Step 5: Think about cause and effect

At B1 level, try to answer not only “What happened?” but also “Why did it happen?” and “What was the result?”

Useful questions:

- Why did Abraham reject idols?
- Why did the people become angry?
- Why did Hagar keep running?
- What lesson does this event teach?

### Step 6: Complete the activities

Do the interactive activities carefully.

Before choosing an answer:

- read the question twice
- look for evidence in the text
- think about the whole paragraph, not only one word

### Step 7: Check feedback

When you finish an activity, read the feedback. If your answer is wrong, return to the text and find the reason.

Mistakes are not failure. They show you what to review.

### Step 8: Review and produce language

After each section:

- write 4–6 new words
- say the main idea aloud
- write a 2–3 sentence summary
- answer one reflection question

## 6. A SIMPLE STUDY ROUTINE

Small, regular study is better than long, unfocused study.

### A. Short Study Plan (10–15 minutes)

Best for quick review.

**What to do:**

1. Open one short section.
2. Look at the picture and title.
3. Listen once.
4. Read the section.
5. Click 2–3 difficult words.
6. Do one activity.
7. Say one sentence about the lesson.

### B. Normal Study Plan (20–25 minutes)

Best for regular self-study.

**What to do:**

1. Preview the section.
2. Listen and read.
3. Write 4–5 new words.
4. Complete the activities.
5. Correct your mistakes.
6. Retell the section in 3–4 sentences.
7. Write one reflection sentence.

### C. Deep Study Plan (30–40 minutes)

Best for weekend study or revision.

**What to do:**

1. Study one full story block.
2. Listen twice.
3. Read slowly.
4. Learn 6–8 useful words.
5. Complete all activities.
6. Write a short summary.
7. Explain one value from the story.
8. Say your summary aloud.

## 7. STUDY BLOCK 1  
### Abraham and the World of Idols

In this part, Abraham lives in Babylon, where people worship stars, the moon, the sun, and statues. His father makes idols. Abraham sees that these statues cannot eat, drink, speak, move, hear, or help anyone.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What is an idol?
- Why do people sometimes follow wrong traditions?
- Can something made by people be a real god?

### While you read

Look for:

- what people worshipped
- what Abraham noticed about idols
- what his father believed
- why Abraham laughed at the statue of Mardukh
- how Abraham’s heart was different from the people around him

### Key words

idol, statue, temple, worship, bow, beg, favor, respect, hatred, intelligent

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Write 5 words connected to idol worship.
2. Answer: Why did Abraham reject idols?
3. Complete the sentence: “A statue cannot be Allah because …”
4. Retell this part in 3 sentences.

### Self-check

- Can I explain the problem with idol worship?
- Can I describe Abraham’s father’s job?
- Can I use words like worship, statue, and temple correctly?

### Reflection

- Why is it important to question wrong ideas?
- How can a person stay sincere when many people around them are wrong?

---

## 8. STUDY BLOCK 2  
### Abraham Discovers the Creator

In this part, Abraham looks at the star, the moon, and the sun. He understands that anything that appears, disappears, rises, and sets cannot be Allah. Allah is not one of the creations. Allah is the Creator of everything.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What does “Creator” mean?
- Why are the star, moon, and sun not Allah?
- How can thinking carefully lead a person to truth?

### While you read

Look for:

- what Abraham sees in the sky
- what he says when the star disappears
- what he understands when the moon fades
- what he learns when the sun sets
- how Allah chooses him to guide people

### Key words

Creator, creation, prostrate, guide, path, fade, disappear, sunrise, sunset, messenger

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Make a table: star / moon / sun / why it cannot be Allah.
2. Write one sentence with Creator.
3. Answer: Why did Abraham ask Allah for guidance?
4. Say the main idea aloud.

### Self-check

- Can I explain the difference between the Creator and creation?
- Can I explain Abraham’s reasoning?
- Can I use because and however to connect ideas?

### Reflection

- Why is guidance important?
- What does this part teach about using the mind and heart together?

---

## 9. STUDY BLOCK 3  
### Calling People, Breaking the Idols, and the Fire

In this part, Abraham speaks to his father and people. He calls them to worship Allah alone. Later, he breaks the idols to show that they are powerless. The people become angry and throw him into a huge fire, but Allah makes the fire cool and safe for him.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- Is it easy to tell the truth when people disagree?
- What does courage mean?
- What does trust in Allah mean?

### While you read

Look for:

- how Abraham speaks to his father
- why the people ignore him
- why he leaves the biggest statue untouched
- how the people react
- how Allah saves Abraham

### Key words

furious, arrogant, ashamed, axe, destroy, untouched, punish, firewood, flames, miracle, trust

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Put the events in order.
2. Write 5 past simple verbs from this part.
3. Answer: Why was Abraham brave?
4. Explain the miracle in 2 sentences.
5. Write one sentence about trust in Allah.

### Self-check

- Can I retell the idol-breaking event?
- Can I explain why the biggest idol was left untouched?
- Can I use past tense verbs correctly?
- Can I explain the fire miracle without giving unnecessary details?

### Reflection

- Can a person be calm in a very difficult situation?
- Why do some people refuse the truth even when they understand it?

---

## 10. STUDY BLOCK 4  
### Abraham and Nimrod, Then Migration

In this part, Nimrod claims that he can give life and death. Abraham answers with clear logic: Allah makes the sun rise in the east, so Nimrod should make it rise from the west if he has power. Nimrod cannot answer. Later, Abraham leaves Babylon to spread Allah’s message.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What is real power?
- Why do arrogant people sometimes misunderstand power?
- Why might a prophet leave his homeland?

### While you read

Look for:

- what Nimrod claims
- how Abraham answers him
- why Nimrod becomes angrier
- who believes with Abraham
- why Abraham decides to leave Babylon

### Key words

king, ordinary, death, life, power, response, naturally, faith, migration, message

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Write Nimrod’s claim in one sentence.
2. Write Abraham’s answer in one sentence.
3. Answer: Why was Abraham’s argument strong?
4. Write 3 sentences about migration for Allah’s message.

### Self-check

- Can I explain the debate with Nimrod?
- Can I describe the difference between human power and Allah’s power?
- Can I explain why Abraham migrated?

### Reflection

- Why is clear thinking important in a debate?
- What can people sacrifice for truth?

## 11. STUDY BLOCK 5  
### Hagar, Ishmael, Zamzam, Mecca, and the Ka’ba

In this part, Abraham leaves Hagar and Ishmael in a lonely valley by Allah’s command. Hagar trusts Allah and searches for water. Allah gives Zamzam. People settle there, Mecca grows, and later Abraham and Ishmael build the Ka’ba.

### Before you read

Ask yourself:

- What would you do in a desert without water?
- Why is effort important together with trust?
- Why is the Ka’ba important for Muslims?

### While you read

Look for:

- where Hagar and Ishmael stay
- what Abraham prays for
- how Hagar searches for water
- how Zamzam appears
- how Mecca begins to grow
- how Abraham and Ishmael build the Ka’ba

### Key words

valley, desert, protect, blessing, thirst, spring, sacred, settle, foundation, construct, pilgrimage

### After you read

Do these tasks:

1. Draw or describe the order of events.
2. Answer: Why did Hagar run between Safa and Marwa?
3. Say one sentence about Zamzam.
4. Say one sentence about the Ka’ba.
5. Write one lesson about patience and effort.

### Self-check

- Can I explain the story of Zamzam?
- Can I describe Hagar’s effort?
- Can I explain why Mecca became important?
- Can I connect Hajj with Abraham’s family?

### Reflection

- Why should people combine trust in Allah with effort?
- Why is gratitude important after receiving a blessing?

## 12. HOW TO LEARN NEW WORDS

At B1 level, you should learn words in groups and use them in sentences.

### Vocabulary steps

1. Read the word.
2. Guess the meaning from context.
3. Check the meaning.
4. Notice the sentence.
5. Say the word aloud.
6. Write your own sentence.
7. Review it later.

### Example

**Word:** arrogant  
**Meaning:** thinking you are better than others  
**My sentence:** The people were arrogant because they knew Abraham was right but refused to accept the truth.

### Useful word groups from this book

**Belief and worship:** worship, idol, statue, Creator, messenger, faith, Tawheed  
**Feelings:** furious, ashamed, calm, hopeful, grateful, sincere  
**Actions:** guide, destroy, punish, protect, migrate, construct, settle  
**Values:** courage, trust, patience, gratitude, sincerity, obedience

## 13. HOW TO SPEAK DURING SELF-STUDY

Even when you study alone, speak aloud.

After each section, try one of these:

- retell the event in 3 sentences
- explain one value
- compare two characters
- answer one “why” question
- summarize the part in your own words

### Useful sentence starters

- This part is about …
- Abraham understood that …
- The people became angry because …
- Allah helped Abraham when …
- Hagar showed patience because …
- The lesson of this part is …
- In my opinion, this event teaches …

---

## 14. HOW TO WRITE DURING SELF-STUDY

You do not need very long writing every time. Short, clear writing is useful.

### Easy writing tasks

- write 4 new words and sentences
- write a 3-sentence summary
- explain one cause and one result
- describe one character’s feeling
- write one lesson from the story

### B1 writing prompts

- Why did Abraham reject idol worship?
- How did Abraham use logic when speaking to his people?
- Why was the fire miracle important?
- How did Hagar show both trust and effort?
- What does the building of the Ka’ba teach Muslims?

---

## 15. HOW TO CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

After each block, ask yourself:

- Can I say what happened?
- Can I explain why it happened?
- Can I name the main people?
- Can I use 5 key words?
- Can I retell the part in order?
- Can I explain one lesson?

If your answer is “not yet,” go back, listen again, and reread the most important paragraph.

---

## 16. FINAL REVIEW TASKS

When you finish the book, complete these tasks.

### Vocabulary review

Choose 15 important words and write one sentence for each.

### Story timeline

Write the main events in order:

1. Abraham grows up in Babylon.
2. He rejects idols.
3. He discovers that Allah is the Creator.
4. He calls his father and people.
5. He breaks the idols.
6. Allah saves him from the fire.
7. He debates Nimrod.
8. He migrates.
9. Hagar and Ishmael stay in the valley.
10. Zamzam appears.
11. Mecca grows.
12. Abraham and Ishmael build the Ka’ba.

### Speaking review

Tell the story in 2 minutes.

### Writing review

Write one paragraph about this question:

**What is the most important lesson from the story of Prophet Abraham?**
