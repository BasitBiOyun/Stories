# Stories Content Quality Specification

## Status

Version: 0.1 — foundation draft

This document is the single source of truth for reviewing and regenerating derived learning material in Stories. It applies to English and Arabic A2, B1, and B2 books.

## Non-negotiable boundary

Content-quality work must not change canonical material:

- story prose
- chapter titles, boundaries, order, or page identity
- approved narration audio or audio references
- quotations and source references embedded in story pages

Only derived material may be reviewed or regenerated:

- vocabulary and glossary material
- chapter exercises
- Quick Challenges
- Final Challenges
- correct and incorrect feedback
- teacher guides
- self-study guides
- achievement and summary material

Every derived item must be supported by the unchanged canonical text of the same book. Information that is absent from the canonical text must not be introduced as fact inside an exercise or guide.

## Quality objectives

Every book must be:

1. Text-grounded: answers and teaching claims are recoverable from the canonical book.
2. CEFR-appropriate: language, reasoning demand, instructions, and expected output match the declared level.
3. Pedagogically varied: exercises test more than simple recall while remaining suitable for the level.
4. Consistent: instructions, answer data, feedback, ids, and guide structure follow one shared standard.
5. Respectful: religious, historical, and cultural material is handled without speculation, ridicule, sensationalism, or unsupported interpretation.
6. Classroom-ready: activities can be used by a teacher without reconstructing missing objectives, answers, timing, or differentiation.
7. Self-study-ready: independent learners receive clear instructions, useful feedback, and realistic next steps.

## Evidence rule

Before creating an item, identify its evidence in one or more canonical story pages. The item must not depend on outside knowledge.

Allowed:

- direct detail stated in the text
- sequence explicitly established by the text
- cause or motivation explicitly stated or strongly supported by the text
- level-appropriate inference using evidence contained in the text
- values reflection clearly labelled as reflection rather than factual recall

Not allowed:

- adding a religious or historical claim because it is commonly known elsewhere
- treating a disputed interpretation as an uncontested answer
- asking for a detail that appears only in another level or language version
- inventing quotations, motives, dates, names, relationships, or outcomes
- creating distractors that introduce harmful or misleading claims without clear correction

## CEFR standard

### A2

Primary demand:

- direct comprehension
- basic sequence
- concrete vocabulary
- people, places, actions, and clearly stated reasons
- short instructions and short learner responses

Language rules:

- prefer common words and short clauses
- avoid unnecessary abstraction, idioms, and complex negatives
- keep distractors clearly distinct but not absurd
- inference must be minimal and strongly signposted by the text

Expected production:

- one word, phrase, simple sentence, ordering, matching, or short personal reflection

### B1

Primary demand:

- cause and result
- motivation
- comparison of events or choices
- contextual vocabulary and collocations
- short explanations and summaries

Language rules:

- use natural intermediate sentence structures
- distractors may be plausible but must remain text-grounded
- include a balanced mix of recall and interpretation
- avoid questions answerable only through obscure wording differences

Expected production:

- connected sentences, short explanations, short summaries, evidence selection, and guided reflection

### B2

Primary demand:

- inference
- comparison
- evaluation
- synthesis across chapters
- evidence-based interpretation
- nuanced and topic-specific vocabulary

Language rules:

- use natural upper-intermediate language without artificial complexity
- distractors should represent credible misunderstandings of the text
- distinguish clearly between textual evidence and open reflection
- questions must not reward guessing based on general religious or historical knowledge

Expected production:

- justified answers, comparison, synthesis, interpretation, and structured reflection using textual evidence

## Exercise design standard

### Required properties for every scored exercise

- unique stable id
- valid exercise type
- concise title
- unambiguous instructions
- one clearly defined task
- valid answer data
- explanation when the answer is not self-evident
- useful correct feedback
- useful incorrect feedback

Incorrect feedback must help the learner reconsider the relevant idea without simply revealing the full answer. Empty, generic, or punitive feedback is not acceptable.

### Exercise variety

A complete book should combine, where supported by the interface and level:

- true/false
- multiple choice
- matching
- sequencing
- fill in the blanks
- drag and drop
- tap reveal
- reflection or discussion
- quiz game

No book should rely almost entirely on one question type. Reflection items are unscored and must not be counted as objective questions unless the interface explicitly treats them as such.

### Multiple-choice rules

- exactly one correct answer
- normally three or four options
- all options use parallel grammar and comparable length where practical
- no option such as all of the above unless pedagogically necessary
- no trick based on tiny wording differences
- distractors must be plausible within the chapter context but clearly false according to the text
- the correct option must not be identifiable only because it is longer or more detailed

### True/false rules

- one claim per item
- no double negatives
- false statements must be clearly correctable from the text
- avoid statements that are partly true and partly false

### Sequencing rules

- each item represents a distinct event
- the canonical text establishes the order
- wording must not reveal the order through numbering or temporal labels unless those labels are the learning target

### Matching rules

- pairs must use one consistent relationship, such as word–meaning, person–action, event–result, or concept–example
- avoid overlapping answers
- keep both columns grammatically consistent

### Fill-in-the-blanks rules

- the missing element must be recoverable from context
- do not remove multiple words that create several equally valid answers
- grammar and spelling expectations must be clear

### Reflection rules

- label the task as reflection, discussion, or personal response
- do not assign a single factual correct answer
- provide a model direction or success criterion rather than marking a personal response wrong
- keep values prompts connected to the story rather than turning them into generic moral slogans

## Challenge standard

### Quick Challenges

A Quick Challenge should review the chapter or a clearly defined chapter group. It should be short, varied, and focused on the most important comprehension and language outcomes.

Recommended range:

- A2: 2–4 objective items or one compact interactive activity
- B1: 3–5 items with at least one cause, sequence, or contextual vocabulary task
- B2: 3–5 items with at least one inference, comparison, or evidence task

### Final Challenges

Exact target counts:

- A2: 8 objective questions
- B1: 9 objective questions
- B2: 10 objective questions

The count refers to scored objective questions. Reflection prompts may accompany the challenge but do not replace the required objective questions.

Recommended blueprint:

#### A2 — 8 questions

- 3 direct comprehension
- 1 sequence
- 2 vocabulary in context
- 1 cause or simple lesson
- 1 whole-story review

#### B1 — 9 questions

- 2 direct comprehension
- 2 cause, result, or motivation
- 1 sequence or chronology
- 2 vocabulary or collocation
- 1 comparison or short inference
- 1 whole-story synthesis

#### B2 — 10 questions

- 2 evidence-based comprehension
- 2 inference or motivation
- 1 comparison
- 1 chronology or development
- 2 nuanced vocabulary or meaning in context
- 1 evaluation of an idea using textual evidence
- 1 whole-story synthesis

Questions should cover the book rather than concentrating on the final chapters.

## Vocabulary standard

Vocabulary must be selected from the canonical text or be directly necessary to understand it.

Each selected item should:

- be useful at the declared CEFR level
- have a definition simpler than or equal to the target level
- use the intended meaning from the chapter
- include an example that does not introduce unsupported story facts
- avoid duplicate entries unless deliberate recycling is documented

A2 prioritizes concrete and high-frequency words. B1 includes useful collocations and contextual meanings. B2 includes nuanced, academic, and topic-specific language that genuinely appears in or supports the text.

## Teacher guide standard

Each teacher-guide section must map to a named chapter or an explicitly labelled whole-book stage.

A chapter-linked section must include:

- realistic timing
- measurable objectives
- reading, language, or values focus supported by the chapter
- a usable lesson sequence
- discussion prompts
- support for struggling learners
- extension for fast finishers
- formative assessment or exit evidence

The guide must distinguish between:

- facts stated by the canonical text
- interpretation supported by the text
- open classroom reflection

External links or factual extensions require separate human verification and must not be silently presented as canonical story content.

## Self-study guide standard

A self-study guide must tell the learner:

- what to do before reading
- how to use narration and text together
- how to record vocabulary
- how to check exercises and use feedback
- what to do after a weak result
- how to review the whole book

Instructions must be achievable without a teacher. A self-study section must not refer to unavailable worksheets, classroom groups, or teacher-only actions.

## English–Arabic relationship

English and Arabic materials should pursue equivalent learning objectives for the same story and level. They do not need to be literal translations when natural language or pedagogy requires adaptation.

Required alignment:

- same covered chapters
- same Final Challenge objective-question count
- comparable cognitive demand
- equivalent answerability from the respective canonical text
- comparable guide coverage

A fact may not be copied from one language version into the other unless it is present in that language's canonical story text.

## Religious, historical, and cultural safeguards

- preserve the terminology and framing used by the canonical source
- do not invent dialogue or divine speech
- do not present speculation as revelation or established history
- avoid visual or verbal trivialisation of sacred subjects
- do not turn complex moral events into mocking or game-like punishment language
- avoid claiming that one personal reflection is the only acceptable moral response
- flag uncertainty for human review instead of silently resolving it

## Review rubric

Each regenerated book is scored out of 100:

- canonical grounding: 25
- CEFR appropriateness: 20
- question and feedback quality: 20
- coverage and variety: 15
- guide usability and alignment: 10
- English–Arabic parity: 5
- religious, historical, and cultural care: 5

Minimum release threshold: 85/100, with no failure in canonical grounding or safeguards.

## Required workflow

1. Lock and verify the canonical baseline.
2. Inventory the current derived material and validator warnings.
3. Produce an evidence map from chapters to learning objectives.
4. Draft English derived material for one pilot book.
5. Run structural validators and a manual pedagogical review.
6. Adapt the Arabic material from its own canonical text and the approved objectives.
7. Run parity checks.
8. Conduct teacher review and record requested changes.
9. Freeze the approved pilot as the gold-standard example.
10. Process remaining books level by level in separate, reviewable batches.

## Pilot

The first pilot is `adam:A2`.

Pilot scope:

- English vocabulary, chapter exercises, Quick Challenges, Final Challenge, teacher guide, and self-study guide
- Arabic equivalent after English structure and objectives are approved
- no canonical story or narration change

The pilot must be approved before bulk regeneration begins.
