## Automated baseline findings

- Final Challenge is missing or contains no questions.

These warnings are accurate but do not capture all pedagogical issues below.

## Findings by severity

### Critical

#### 1. The actual Final Challenge is empty

Page 16 is typed as `final-challenge` but contains no exercises. The separate Final Review page contains activities and a seven-question quiz game, but the application and validator correctly treat the Final Challenge as missing.

Required correction:

- page 16 must contain exactly 8 scored objective questions for A2
- reflection prompts must remain outside the objective count
- questions must cover the whole book

#### 2. Some scored material is not securely grounded in this canonical A2 text

Examples:

- Knowledge Check question `q4` states that Allah taught Adam "the names of all things". The A2 canonical text says Adam received knowledge and learned more than the angels, but it does not use this exact factual detail.
- Final quiz hint for Hawwa says her name means "living one". This information is absent from the canonical A2 text.
- Final quiz question 7 asks for "the message of all prophets". The canonical A2 book describes Adam's message, not a complete doctrine about every prophet.
- Knowledge Check question 6 explains acceptance using "sincere and pious". Sincerity is supported; the added term and general rule are not stated in this A2 text.

Required correction:

Every objective item must be answerable from this book without outside religious knowledge.

#### 3. Guide coverage does not map to the book

The teacher guide compresses 10 story chapters into three broad sections:

- Creation & Knowledge
- arrogance vs Humility
- Habil and Qabil

This leaves Life on Earth, The First Messenger, the Paradise sequence, the mistake and regret sequence, and the concluding chapter without clear chapter-level planning.

Required correction:

Create one clearly mapped teacher-guide section per story chapter, or an explicitly documented module map that still covers all 10 chapters. The pilot target is 10 chapter-linked sections.

### High

#### 4. The Knowledge Check lacks type variety

All six Knowledge Check items are true/false. Several repeat information already tested on chapter pages.

Consequences:

- guessing probability is high
- vocabulary, sequence, and cause are under-assessed
- repeated statements add volume without adding evidence of learning

Required correction:

Use four or five mixed items in the Knowledge Check, such as multiple choice, matching, sequencing, and one true/false item.

#### 5. Chapter exercise alignment is inconsistent

Page 6, Life on Earth, contains a fill-in-the-blank exercise about the warning in Paradise. The answer is valid elsewhere in the story, but the task does not assess the learning focus of page 6.

Required correction:

Each chapter exercise should primarily assess its own chapter. Deliberate spiral review must be labelled and balanced rather than appearing accidental.

#### 6. The glossary is described as complete but omits later vocabulary

The two glossary pages contain 24 entries, apparently covering chapters 1–8. Vocabulary from chapters 9 and 10, including `crow`, `panic`, `digging`, `jealousy`, and `worldwide`, is absent despite the label "complete list".

Required correction:

Either include all approved high-value vocabulary or rename and redesign the glossary as a selected vocabulary review.

#### 7. Several definitions are too difficult, inaccurate, or poorly matched to A2

Examples:

- `creature`: the chapter definition says "An animal or person, especially as distinct from a human," which is internally confusing and unsuitable for the intended context.
- `planet`: the astronomical definition "a celestial body moving in an orbit around a star" is much harder than the story use and unnecessary at A2.
- `arrogant`, `wonderful`, `warning`, and several glossary definitions use abstract wording above the surrounding A2 style.

Required correction:

Definitions should be shorter, concrete, contextual, and no harder than the target word where possible.

### Medium

#### 8. Exercise distribution is narrow

The chapter pages rely heavily on true/false and multiple choice. Only isolated pages use tap reveal, fill in the blanks, or drag and drop.

Required correction:

Use a deliberate type distribution that matches chapter content rather than repeating the easiest implementation.

#### 9. Feedback quality is uneven

Most chapter feedback is usable, but some text is generic or introduces claims beyond the immediate evidence. The reflection activity has empty incorrect feedback even though personal reflection should not be treated as wrong.

Required correction:

- objective incorrect feedback should direct the learner back to a chapter idea
- reflection should use a success criterion or model direction, not incorrect marking
- explanations must not add unsupported facts

#### 10. Values categorisation is sometimes oversimplified

The Final Review sorts behaviours into broad GOOD and BAD categories. This is accessible, but terms such as regret and anger require more care: regret may follow wrongdoing and anger is a feeling whose actions matter. Binary categorisation can flatten the lesson.

Required correction:

At A2, keep language simple but classify actions precisely. Prefer categories such as "helpful actions / harmful actions" or "good choices / harmful choices" when the story supports them.

#### 11. Self-study sections retain teacher-oriented structures

The chapter-linked self-study guide uses fields such as `fastFinishers` and `strugglingLearners`, and includes instructions referring to charts or animations that may not be directly available to an independent learner.

Required correction:

Rewrite each section as independent actions: standard task, easier support route, optional extension, and self-check.

#### 12. Teacher guide references resources that are not guaranteed to exist

The guide lists worksheets such as sequencing sheets, Venn diagrams, and comparative-adjective practice without confirming that those resources are supplied.

Required correction:

Either include the resource, describe a no-print classroom alternative, or remove the reference.

## Material worth preserving

The current material also has useful foundations:

- every canonical chapter already has at least one exercise slot
- instructions are generally short and understandable
- most correct answers are structurally valid
- the story naturally supports sequence, vocabulary, behaviour, cause, regret, and whole-story review
- the general self-study text contains practical reading, listening, vocabulary, and error-review advice
- the teacher guide already includes timing, objectives, differentiation, assessment, and values prompts

The pilot should refine and reorganise these strengths rather than discard them blindly.

## Proposed Adam A2 English blueprint

### Chapter exercises

| Chapter | Primary learning focus | Proposed main type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adam's creation from soil | True/false with evidence feedback |
| 2 | Knowledge and respect | Multiple choice |
| 3 | Iblis's reasoning and arrogance | Matching idea to character |
| 4 | Paradise and the warning | Tap reveal or direct comprehension |
| 5 | Lie, forgetting, mistake, regret | Sequencing |
| 6 | Responsibilities on Earth | Fill in the blanks or matching |
| 7 | Adam's teaching and message | Multiple choice |
| 8 | Habil and Qabil's work and offerings | Matching |
| 9 | Threat, harm, regret, and the crow | Sequencing |
| 10 | Whole-story lessons | Guided reflection with success criteria |

### Knowledge Check

Target: 4 mixed objective items.

Suggested coverage:

- creation and knowledge
- Paradise warning and mistake
- life on Earth and Adam's teaching
- Habil, Qabil, and the crow

Suggested types:

- 1 multiple choice
- 1 sequencing
- 1 matching
- 1 true/false

### Vocabulary Challenge

Target: 6–8 high-value items selected across the whole story.

Selection should prioritise words that are:

- present in the canonical text
- useful at A2
- central to comprehension
- not already obvious cognates or low-value labels

### Final Review & Reflection

Keep this page unscored or lightly scored. Proposed activities:

1. whole-story sequence
2. characters and actions matching
3. helpful and harmful choices classification
4. one guided reflection with sentence starters

This page should prepare learners for the Final Challenge rather than duplicate it.

### Final Challenge — exactly 8 objective questions

Proposed distribution:

1. direct comprehension: creation
2. direct comprehension: knowledge and respect
3. direct comprehension: Paradise warning
4. sequence: warning, mistake, regret, Earth
5. vocabulary in context: arrogance or regret
6. vocabulary in context: shepherd, farmer, or offering
7. simple cause or lesson: Habil and Qabil
8. whole-story review: Adam's teaching and the final message

At least four different interaction or question forms should be represented where the existing interface permits.

### Teacher guide

Target: 10 chapter-linked sections.

Each section should contain:

- 25–35 minute realistic timing
- two or three measurable A2 objectives
- 3–5 target words
- one reading/listening routine
- one chapter exercise or no-print classroom activity
- support route
- extension route
- one assessment check
- one carefully framed values question where appropriate

### Self-study guide

Target:

- retain a shorter general study guide
- add one concise self-study routine for each of the 10 chapters
- replace teacher-oriented differentiation labels with `Support` and `Extension`
- include a simple recovery rule after wrong answers
- include a final review routine linked to the eight-question Final Challenge

## Decision

Adam A2 English is suitable as the pilot because its problems represent the main platform-wide categories:

- empty or miswired Final Challenge
- incomplete feedback
- over-reliance on simple question types
- unsupported factual extensions
- vocabulary level mismatch
- guide-to-chapter misalignment
- self-study material that is not fully independent
