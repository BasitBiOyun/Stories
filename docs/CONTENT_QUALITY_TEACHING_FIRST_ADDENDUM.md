# Teaching-First Question Design Addendum

This addendum records teacher feedback accepted for the Stories content-quality system. It supplements `docs/CONTENT_QUALITY_SPEC.md` and takes precedence where the older challenge counts or question-purpose wording differs.

## Core principle

Questions in Stories are not designed only to measure performance. Their purpose changes with the reading stage.

### While reading

The main goal is **teaching and reinforcement**.

- Quick Challenges help learners notice, retrieve, and rebuild information from the chapter they have just read.
- At A2 and B1, this formative role is essential.
- A wrong answer should normally trigger evidence-finding, rereading, and a second attempt.
- The learner should leave the activity knowing the information more clearly than before.

### Post reading

The goal is **retrieval, organisation, and consolidation**.

- Knowledge Checks and Review Challenges revisit important information.
- Questions should match the discourse and style of the source. For an informative narrative/essay-like text, tasks should focus on stated information, sequence, comparison, cause/result, vocabulary in context, and text-supported conclusions.
- Do not create a game-show style simply to make questions feel difficult.

### Final Challenge

The goal is **whole-text recall with appropriate level demand**.

- Final questions should remind learners of the most important information across the whole text.
- All levels use exactly **10 objective Final Challenge questions**.
- Print layout uses **5 + 5** questions.
- A2 remains direct and concrete.
- B1 may use cause/result, motivation, and short interpretation while remaining reinforcement-oriented.
- B2 may combine reinforcement, measurement, evidence, inference, evaluation, and genuine challenge.

## Review Challenge count

All levels use exactly **8 Review Challenge questions**.

For A2 print:

- page 1: questions 1–4
- page 2: questions 5–8

The same content count applies to B1 and B2 so the publication template remains stable.

## Informative-text alignment

Question style must fit the source text.

Preferred operations include:

- find a stated fact
- identify the main information in a paragraph
- order events or stages
- connect a person with stated information
- identify a stated cause/result
- compare two people, events, or ideas using textual evidence
- complete a key sentence or idea
- retrieve vocabulary meaning in context
- summarise a short section

Avoid:

- artificial trick questions
- outside factual knowledge
- unsupported moral claims
- challenge wording that is harder than the reading itself without a learning reason
- distractors that introduce new religious or historical claims
- scoring language that makes while-reading practice feel like a high-stakes test

## Feedback standard

Useful incorrect feedback should normally do one or more of the following:

- name the chapter/paragraph to reread
- point to the type of evidence to find
- remind the learner of the comparison or sequence involved
- invite a second attempt after rereading

It should not simply say `Wrong` or immediately reveal a long answer.

## Guide alignment

Teacher Guide and Self-Study Guide instructions should explicitly distinguish:

- **before reading:** orient and predict
- **while reading:** notice and reinforce
- **after reading:** retrieve, organise, summarise, reflect
- **final review:** recall the whole text and identify gaps for rereading

This reading-stage distinction is part of the product’s pedagogical identity.
