# B2 Teacher Guide: The Story of Prophet Adam

## For Teachers of Grades 11–12 English Classes

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

### Purpose of the Book
This interactive digital storybook is designed to help upper-secondary learners develop advanced reading, listening, speaking, and reflective thinking skills through the story of Prophet Adam. At B2 level, the aim is not only to understand the sequence of events, but also to analyze ideas, compare perspectives, interpret values, and discuss the relationship between knowledge, moral responsibility, human weakness, and guidance.

The guide supports teachers in turning the story into a structured learning experience that combines:

- literary and thematic reading
- academic vocabulary development
- analytical discussion
- reflective writing
- values-based interpretation
- digital interaction through audio, hotspots, visuals, and tasks

### Target Learners
- Primary: Turkish 11th and 12th grade students studying English at approximately CEFR B2.
- Secondary: Other upper-intermediate learners who can discuss abstract ideas in English with structured support.

### Language Level
**CEFR B2**

Learners at this level should be able to:

- follow longer texts with layered meaning
- discuss causes, consequences, and moral tensions
- compare viewpoints and explain interpretations
- use more precise thematic vocabulary
- respond in short analytical paragraphs and spoken discussion

### Main Learning Focus
- Reading for meaning, implication, and theme
- Listening and note-taking through audio support
- Academic and thematic vocabulary in context
- Analytical speaking and extended responses
- Structured paragraph and reflection writing
- Values and ethical reasoning
- Digital reading support for deeper comprehension

### Educational Value
The B2 version of the story gives teachers a strong framework for discussing major human questions: origin, value, knowledge, obedience, arrogance, repentance, vicegerency, moral struggle, and the contrast between good and evil. The text also supports deeper classroom inquiry into:

- why knowledge gives responsibility
- how arrogance distorts judgment
- why repentance preserves human dignity
- how revelation and human reason complement each other
- how jealousy and anger destroy both individual and social life

### Digital Support
The digital format is especially valuable at B2 because it allows students to move between surface understanding and deeper interpretation.

- Hotspots support difficult vocabulary and concepts.
- Audio supports rhythm, phrasing, and comprehension of longer clauses.
- Interactive checks help teachers monitor understanding before moving into abstract discussion.
- Visuals help anchor theological and moral ideas in memorable imagery.

## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT

This guide supports an integrated upper-secondary English classroom by combining language learning, interpretive reading, guided discussion, and ethical reflection.

### Language Skills
- **Listening & Viewing:** students listen for detail, emphasis, and tone while following the text.
- **Reading Comprehension:** students move from literal understanding to interpretation, evaluation, and thematic analysis.
- **Spoken Interaction:** students justify opinions, compare ideas, and build longer spoken responses.
- **Vocabulary Development:** students use abstract and semi-academic words in context, discussion, and writing.
- **Writing Development:** students produce short analytical paragraphs, comparisons, and reflective responses.

### Personal and Intellectual Development
- **Values Education:** humility, accountability, sincerity, justice, patience, responsibility, repentance, self-control.
- **Social-Emotional Learning:** recognizing inner conflict, moral struggle, regret, and consequences.
- **Reflective Thinking:** connecting revealed guidance, human freedom, and ethical choices.
- **Critical Thinking:** distinguishing between appearance and truth, claim and evidence, pride and dignity, knowledge and misuse of knowledge.

## 3. TEACHING APPROACH

The teaching approach at B2 should remain interactive and supportive, but it should also challenge students to think, explain, and justify.

### Core Principles
- Pre-teach only the most necessary terms, not every difficult word.
- Move from comprehension to interpretation.
- Use text evidence when discussing major ideas.
- Encourage pair and small-group discussion before whole-class response.
- Let students compare characters, motives, and consequences.
- Use the story to build analytical language, not only moral summary.
- Keep teacher talk purposeful and discussion-oriented.

### Recommended Classroom Sequence
1. **Encounter it** – students observe the visual/textual entry point.
2. **Understand it** – students secure literal meaning.
3. **Analyze it** – students examine motives, contrasts, and themes.
4. **Express it** – students discuss or write using evidence.
5. **Apply it** – students connect the lesson to ethical and social realities.

### Teacher Role
The teacher should function as a facilitator of interpretation. At B2, students should not only be told the lesson. They should be guided to identify it, defend it, and connect it to broader ideas. The teacher’s job is to scaffold precision, deepen discussion, and keep abstract talk rooted in the text.

## 5. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER READING FRAMEWORK

### Before Reading
- Use the title, cover, or major visual for prediction.
- Teach 5–8 key terms needed for entry into the chapter.
- Ask one factual and one philosophical anticipation question.
- Activate prior knowledge without allowing discussion to become vague or overly broad.

### During Reading
- Read or play the text in meaningful sections.
- Pause at major turning points.
- Ask students to identify the speaker, claim, motive, or consequence.
- Encourage them to infer meaning before using word support.
- Recycle target language through oral response.
- Ask students to mark lines that show contrast, warning, regret, or insight.

### After Reading
- Retell the events with clear sequence.
- Move to interpretation and evaluation.
- Ask students to support answers with evidence from the text.
- End with a short spoken or written reflection.
- Use one exit task to check language and one to check deeper understanding.

## 6. CHAPTER SUPPORT (40-MINUTE LESSONS)

> These three instructional modules are the B2 teaching blocks for the Adam unit. They are designed for upper-secondary learners who can move beyond summary toward interpretation, evaluation, and thematic discussion.

### Chapter 1: Creation, Vicegerency & Knowledge

**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain the idea of human vicegerency in clear English
- analyze why knowledge is central to Adam’s value in the story
- distinguish between outward origin and inner worth
- use precise thematic vocabulary in discussion
- interpret the contrast between the angels’ response and Iblis’s response

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:

- define vicegerency in simple but accurate terms
- explain why Adam is honored in the narrative
- discuss the relationship between knowledge and responsibility
- state why superiority is not based on race, color, or origin

#### Key Vocabulary
vicegerent, stewardship, revelation, intellect, dignity, humility, superiority, creature, responsibility, obedience

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use concept-based teaching. This chapter introduces foundational ideas that should not remain at the level of plot. Students need to understand that the text connects human value with knowledge, moral ability, and responsibility rather than material origin. Encourage them to paraphrase difficult ideas before discussing them.

#### Grammar / Language Focus
- defining and explaining abstract concepts
- cause and effect language
- contrast markers: however, whereas, while, on the other hand
- relative clauses for explanation

#### Pronunciation Focus
- stress in multi-syllabic academic words: *responsibility, superiority, revelation, obedience*
- sentence stress when expressing contrast

#### Materials / App Features
- opening pages of the B2 story
- hotspots on creation, knowledge, and angels’ prostration
- audio playback
- concept board or digital note map

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–5 min | Opening Question**
- Ask: “What gives a human being value?”
- Collect 3–4 brief student responses.

**5–10 min | Visual and Concept Entry**
- Show the opening visual or first page.
- Introduce vicegerent, knowledge, and dignity.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading / Listening**
- Read or play the section on creation, knowledge, and the angels.
- Pause after each key paragraph.

**18–25 min | Text-Based Comprehension**
- Ask students to identify what Adam was given and why it matters.
- Clarify the difference between being created from clay and being honored.

**25–32 min | Pair Analysis**
- Students answer: “Why does the story reject false superiority?”
- Require one text-based example in the answer.

**32–37 min | Discussion**
- Ask: “How can knowledge be a source of both honor and responsibility?”

**37–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one sentence defining vicegerency.
- Write one sentence explaining why origin does not equal superiority.

#### Teacher Language
- “Find the line that supports your answer.”
- “What is the difference between origin and value here?”
- “Can you explain that in clearer English?”
- “Which word in the text shows responsibility?”
- “What idea is being rejected in this passage?”

#### Comprehension Questions
- What was Adam created for in the story?
- Why was Adam taught “the names”?
- Why does Iblis reject Adam?
- What makes Adam valuable according to the text?

#### Discussion Points
- Why is knowledge presented as a trust rather than only an advantage?
- What is wrong with judging superiority by origin?
- How does this chapter challenge racism and arrogance?

#### Possible Difficulties
- Students may understand the events but not the abstract concept of vicegerency.
- Some may confuse “honor” with unrestricted power.
- Students may use moral opinions without textual support.

#### Support Strategies
- Ask students to paraphrase difficult passages in simpler English.
- Use a comparison chart: **origin / gift / response / lesson**.
- Model one evidence-based answer before independent discussion.

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** write a short paragraph on the duties of a vicegerent on earth.
- **Struggling learners:** match concepts to definitions and complete sentence frames.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the knowledge hotspot to move from concrete vocabulary to abstract meaning.
- Use the angels/Iblis comparison moment to discuss different responses to truth.

#### Assessment Tools
- concept definition
- text-based oral response
- exit ticket on dignity and vicegerency

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Students move to labeled stations such as **knowledge**, **responsibility**, **humility**, and **arrogance**, then explain which one best represents each character and why.

#### Global Citizenship Link
How can the idea that all humans come from one beginning challenge prejudice and claims of superiority?

#### Homework / Extension
- Write a paragraph: “Why is knowledge a responsibility, not only a power?”
- Prepare two quotations or lines from the chapter for the next lesson discussion.

### Chapter 2: Iblis, the Test & Repentance

**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain the moral and psychological difference between Adam’s mistake and Iblis’s rebellion
- analyze how arrogance affects judgment
- discuss the meanings of test, temptation, shame, repentance, and mercy
- compare two opposite responses to wrongdoing
- express interpretation using contrastive and evaluative language

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:

- explain why Adam and Iblis are not judged in the same way
- describe the role of temptation in the narrative
- explain why repentance preserves human dignity
- support a comparison with textual evidence

#### Key Vocabulary
temptation, deception, rebellion, repentance, regret, mercy, arrogance, disobedience, weakness, accountability

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use contrastive moral analysis. Students should see that the story does not erase human weakness, but it presents two radically different responses: one rooted in pride and one rooted in repentance. The lesson should help students distinguish between falling into error and refusing truth.

#### Grammar / Language Focus
- contrastive structures
- evaluative language
- modals for judgment and reflection: should, must, cannot, may
- sentence starters for interpretation

#### Pronunciation Focus
- stress and intonation in evaluative speech
- clear articulation of abstract vocabulary: *repentance, accountability, deception, arrogance*

#### Materials / App Features
- pages about Paradise, the forbidden tree, Iblis’s whispering, Adam’s regret, and descent to earth
- relevant hotspots
- comparison chart or Venn diagram

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–5 min | Review**
- Recap Chapter 1 with two student-led summary sentences.

**5–10 min | Key Language Preview**
- Teach or review temptation, repentance, arrogance, mercy, accountability.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading / Listening**
- Read the section on the forbidden tree and the two responses to wrongdoing.
- Pause at the turning points.

**18–24 min | Comparison Task**
- Students complete a chart: **Adam / Iblis / action / response / consequence**.

**24–30 min | Pair Discussion**
- Prompt: “Why is repentance a strength rather than a weakness?”

**30–35 min | Whole-Class Analysis**
- Discuss the difference between forgetting, falling, and insisting on error.

**35–38 min | Reflection**
- Ask: “What destroys a person more: making a mistake or refusing to admit it?”

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one comparison sentence.
- Write one sentence on why repentance matters.

#### Teacher Language
- “What exactly is the difference between the two responses?”
- “Which line shows regret? Which line shows arrogance?”
- “Can you support that interpretation from the text?”
- “Why is shame followed by repentance here?”
- “Is this only a story event, or a model of human behavior?”

#### Comprehension Questions
- How does Iblis try to deceive Adam and Eve?
- What happens after they eat from the forbidden tree?
- How does Adam respond to his mistake?
- Why does Iblis continue in error?

#### Discussion Points
- What is the difference between weakness and rebellion?
- Why is arrogance called a barrier to truth?
- How does repentance restore moral direction?

#### Possible Difficulties
- Students may reduce the lesson to “one was good, one was bad” without nuance.
- Some may struggle to explain repentance in English.
- Others may summarize events without analyzing the response patterns.

#### Support Strategies
- Use sentence frames such as: “Adam’s mistake was different because…”
- Model one strong comparative answer.
- Ask students to locate a line before giving an interpretation.

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** write a short reflection on the difference between guilt, shame, and repentance.
- **Struggling learners:** complete guided comparison statements using a word bank.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the forbidden tree and whispering sections to discuss temptation gradually, not dramatically.
- Use the regret hotspot to explore emotional language and moral self-awareness.

#### Assessment Tools
- comparative oral response
- chart completion
- exit ticket with interpretation

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Students stand on a response line between two labels, **pride** and **repentance**, and justify where each character belongs using one sentence from the text.

#### Global Citizenship Link
How does refusing to admit a mistake make conflict worse in families, schools, or societies?

#### Homework / Extension
- Write a paragraph: “Why is admitting a mistake a form of strength?”
- Prepare one real-life example in which arrogance blocks learning.

### Chapter 3: Earthly Mission, Habil & Qabil, and the Struggle Between Good and Evil

**Timing:** 40 minutes

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- explain the meaning of Adam’s descent to earth as responsibility rather than humiliation
- analyze the story of Habil and Qabil as a study in sincerity, jealousy, and consequence
- discuss how the text presents the ongoing struggle between good and evil
- sequence and interpret major events with precision
- produce a short analytical response using thematic vocabulary

#### Expected Student Outcomes
Students should be able to:

- explain why earthly life is presented as a moral mission
- compare Habil’s sincerity with Qabil’s jealousy
- identify the consequences of anger without self-control
- connect the story to modern ethical life

#### Key Vocabulary
vicegerency, mission, sincerity, offering, jealousy, consequence, restraint, conflict, burial, guidance, moral struggle

#### Pedagogical Focus
Use ethical and thematic analysis. Students at this level should understand that the descent to earth is not merely a punishment scene; it marks the beginning of human duty, civilization, labor, moral struggle, and prophetic guidance. The Habil and Qabil episode should be taught calmly as a lesson in jealousy, loss of self-control, and the consequences of choosing evil.

#### Grammar / Language Focus
- sequencing and cause-effect language
- conditionals for reflection
- discourse markers for analysis: therefore, consequently, in contrast, as a result
- paragraph organization

#### Pronunciation Focus
- chunking longer analytical sentences
- word stress in *consequence, sincerity, civilization, responsibility*

#### Materials / App Features
- pages on descent to earth, earthly responsibility, prophetic mission, and Habil/Qabil
- sequencing task
- crow/raven hotspot
- comparison board

#### 40-Minute Lesson Flow
**0–5 min | Warm-up**
- Ask: “Is earthly life presented as punishment only, or as responsibility too?”

**5–10 min | Vocabulary and Framing**
- Teach sincerity, jealousy, consequence, guidance, mission.

**10–18 min | Guided Reading**
- Read the sections on life on earth and the story of Habil and Qabil.
- Pause to identify cause and consequence.

**18–24 min | Sequencing and Evidence**
- Students place key events in order.
- They identify one sentence showing sincerity and one showing jealousy.

**24–30 min | Pair Analysis**
- Prompt: “Why was one offering accepted and the other rejected?”
- Require explanation beyond “because it was better.”

**30–35 min | Whole-Class Discussion**
- Discuss how jealousy turns moral weakness into destructive action.

**35–38 min | Reflection**
- Ask: “What does the crow/raven episode teach about shame, learning, and consequence?”

**38–40 min | Exit Ticket**
- Write one moral lesson from Habil and Qabil.
- Write one sentence about the purpose of guidance.

#### Teacher Language
- “What is the cause, and what is the consequence?”
- “Which action reveals sincerity?”
- “Why is this not just a family story?”
- “How does this episode represent a wider human struggle?”
- “Can you explain the lesson more precisely?”

#### Comprehension Questions
- Why are Adam and Eve sent to earth?
- What duties are associated with life on earth?
- Why is Habil’s offering accepted?
- What drives Qabil toward violence?
- What does the raven/crow teach him?

#### Discussion Points
- Why is earthly life described as struggle and trust at the same time?
- What is the difference between giving from the heart and giving outwardly?
- How do jealousy and lack of self-control damage human relationships?
- Why does guidance remain necessary for humanity?

#### Possible Difficulties
- Students may interpret the descent to earth only as punishment.
- Some may discuss Habil and Qabil too generally without textual support.
- The emotional intensity of the episode may cause very short or superficial answers.

#### Support Strategies
- Use a cause/consequence chart.
- Provide paragraph frames for analytical response.
- Keep discussion calm, reflective, and non-graphic.

#### Differentiation
- **Fast finishers:** write a short response on how revelation guides human moral choices.
- **Struggling learners:** use sequence cards and guided sentence frames.

#### Interactive App Tips
- Use the earth/labor sections to discuss responsibility, cultivation, and social duty.
- Use the crow/raven hotspot to highlight consequence, learning, and regret.

#### Assessment Tools
- sequencing accuracy
- cause-effect explanation
- analytical exit response

#### Kinesthetic Activity
Students complete a “moral pathway walk” by moving through event cards labeled **choice**, **intention**, **action**, **consequence**, and **lesson**, explaining each stage aloud.

#### Global Citizenship Link
How do jealousy, insincerity, and uncontrolled anger still threaten communities today, and what role do guidance and self-control play in preventing harm?

#### Homework / Extension
- Write a paragraph: “What does the story suggest about the struggle between good and evil in human life?”
- Prepare one modern example of conflict that could be reduced by sincerity and self-control.

## 7. CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT

### Recommended Use Modes
- **Whole-Class Mode:** teacher projects the text, controls pacing, and selects analytical pause points.
- **Pair Work Mode:** one student summarizes, the other supports with evidence or vocabulary.
- **Seminar Mode:** students discuss a focused thematic question in small groups before reporting back.
- **Exit Reflection Mode:** each lesson closes with a short evidence-based written response.

### Practical Notes
- Keep longer reading passages chunked.
- Do not over-explain before students encounter the text.
- Ask for evidence, not only opinion.
- Give students rehearsal time before demanding extended public responses.
- Use hotspots with purpose, especially for abstract terms.

---

## 8. DIFFERENTIATION

### For Support
- Pre-teach key concepts and notional vocabulary.
- Give sentence frames for interpretation.
- Allow brief L1 clarification for highly abstract concepts.
- Use concept maps and comparison tables.
- Break long answers into staged prompts.

### For Extension
- Ask students to write mini analytical paragraphs.
- Invite them to compare character motives across sections.
- Ask them to relate the text to wider philosophical or social questions.
- Encourage them to evaluate claims such as “knowledge without humility becomes dangerous.”

### For Mixed Ability
- Use pairings with clear roles.
- Keep the core question accessible but allow layered answers.
- Offer optional challenge prompts without making them the default.

## 9. ASSESSMENT & RUBRICS

### Assessment Evidence
- participation in analytical discussion
- use of thematic vocabulary
- quality of interpretation
- ability to support answers with text
- sequencing and cause-effect reasoning
- oral explanation
- reflective or analytical writing

### What to Look For
- Can the student distinguish between event and meaning?
- Can the student use the text to support a claim?
- Can the student explain a moral or theological idea in clear English?
- Can the student compare characters, motives, and outcomes?
- Can the student connect the story to a broader human issue without becoming vague?

### Master Rubric (B2)

| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Good (3) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | Uses thematic vocabulary accurately and flexibly | Uses most key words appropriately | Uses limited or imprecise vocabulary |
| Comprehension | Explains events and deeper meanings clearly | Understands main ideas and some implications | Understands surface events only |
| Analysis | Gives thoughtful interpretation with evidence | Gives relevant ideas with limited support | Gives unsupported or overly simple ideas |
| Grammar & Expression | Produces clear, well-formed extended responses | Makes some errors but meaning stays clear | Errors often limit clarity |
| Reflection | Connects the story to values and human experience insightfully | Gives a reasonable personal or moral connection | Gives minimal or unclear reflection |
| Participation | Contributes actively and constructively | Participates with some prompting | Participates rarely or minimally |

## 13. SENSITIVE NOTES

- Present Adam’s mistake with theological care and educational balance.
- Distinguish clearly between human weakness and arrogant rebellion.
- Handle the death of Habil calmly, with focus on consequence rather than detail.
- Avoid sensational or graphic language.
- Keep classroom discussion respectful, text-centered, and age-appropriate.
- Where theological issues become complex, prioritize clear, careful, non-polemical explanation.

---

## 14. ENGLISH TIPS FOR TEACHERS

- Keep instructions clear, but do not oversimplify content.
- Encourage complete answers, not one-word responses.
- Ask for evidence regularly.
- Recycle target vocabulary across reading, speaking, and writing.
- Use reformulation to improve student answers without discouraging them.
- Use Turkish briefly only when concept clarity truly requires it.
- Return discussion to English as soon as the idea is secured.

### Useful Classroom Language
- “Which line supports that interpretation?”
- “Can you explain the idea more precisely?”
- “What is the cause, and what is the result?”
- “Do you agree with that claim? Why?”
- “Compare the two responses.”
- “Use one word from the text in your answer.”
